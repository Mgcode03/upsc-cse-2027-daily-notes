# BLOCK A — What you are actually fighting (45 minutes)

The Civil Services Examination is not a memory contest of random facts. It is a three-filter machine.

**Filter 1 — Prelims.** Two objective papers on one Sunday. Paper I (GS) is the only paper whose marks decide whether you sit for Mains. Paper II (CSAT) is a gate: 33 percent. If you fail CSAT, a brilliant GS score is wasted. Negative marking is one-third. A guessed wrong answer of 2 marks costs 0.66. Random guessing of four options is mathematically negative in the long run. Elimination of two options changes the expected value.

Recent General cutoffs for GS Paper I out of 200: 75.41 (2023), 87.98 (2024), 92.66 (2025). The 2023 paper was an outlier on the low side. Plan as if you need a safe 110–120 raw in GS-I and a comfortable 90+ in CSAT, not 67.

**Filter 2 — Mains.** Nine descriptive papers. Two language papers are qualifying. Seven papers make 1750 marks. Each paper is 3 hours, 250 marks. The skill is structured argument under time, not dumping everything you know.

Recent General Mains cutoffs out of 1750: 741 (2023), 729 (2024), 739 (2025). Final General cutoffs out of 2025 (Mains + 275 interview): 953, 947, 963 in those three years.

**Filter 3 — Personality Test.** 275 marks. The board tests clarity, balance, intellectual honesty, awareness of your DAF, and whether you can be trusted with public power.

Eligibility snapshot: graduation in any discipline. Age (typical pattern, confirm in 2027 notification): General 21–32 as on 1 August of the exam year, OBC +3, SC/ST +5. Attempts: General 6, OBC 9, SC/ST unlimited till age limit. An attempt is counted when you appear in Prelims.

Services: IAS, IPS, IFS (Foreign), IRS and other Group A and B services. Rank plus preference plus vacancy decides allocation.

# BLOCK B — Indian Constitution: origin, philosophy, and the Preamble (75 minutes)

Polity is the most reliable scoring area in Prelims if concepts are clean. In Mains GS II it is the largest cluster. In Ethics, constitutional values reappear as integrity, impartiality and compassion.

## From Company rule to a Constituent Assembly

Regulating Act 1773 created the Governor-General of Bengal and a Supreme Court at Calcutta. Pitt’s India Act 1784 created a Board of Control. Charter Act 1833 made the Governor-General of Bengal the Governor-General of India. Charter Act 1853 introduced open competition for the civil services in principle.

Government of India Act 1858 ended Company rule. Indian Councils Act 1861 began legislative councils. Indian Councils Act 1892 widened discussion. Morley-Minto Reforms (1909) introduced separate electorates for Muslims. Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms (1919) introduced dyarchy in provinces. Government of India Act 1935 is the largest single source of the present Constitution: provincial autonomy, a proposed federation, Federal Court, three lists of subjects, and the office of Governor with special powers.

The Constituent Assembly first met on 9 December 1946. Dr Sachchidananda Sinha was temporary chairman. Dr Rajendra Prasad became President of the Assembly. The Objective Resolution was moved by Jawaharlal Nehru on 13 December 1946 and adopted on 22 January 1947.

The Drafting Committee was set up on 29 August 1947. Dr B. R. Ambedkar was Chairman. Other members included N. Gopalaswami Ayyangar, Alladi Krishnaswamy Ayyar, K. M. Munshi, Syed Mohammad Saadulla, N. Madhava Rau and T. T. Krishnamachari. The Assembly sat for 2 years 11 months 18 days. The Constitution was adopted on 26 November 1949. Some provisions came into force at once. The rest came into force on 26 January 1950, chosen because 26 January 1930 was Purna Swaraj Day.

Sources in function, not as a parrot list: 1935 Act for federal structure, Governor and emergency; Britain for parliamentary government and rule of law; United States for Fundamental Rights and judicial review flavour; Ireland for Directive Principles; Canada for a strong Centre; original Article 21 “procedure established by law” closer to Japan, later expanded in Maneka Gandhi (1978).

## The Preamble

WE, THE PEOPLE OF INDIA, having solemnly resolved to constitute India into a SOVEREIGN SOCIALIST SECULAR DEMOCRATIC REPUBLIC and to secure to all its citizens:
JUSTICE, social, economic and political;
LIBERTY of thought, expression, belief, faith and worship;
EQUALITY of status and of opportunity;
and to promote among them all
FRATERNITY assuring the dignity of the individual and the unity and integrity of the Nation;
IN OUR CONSTITUENT ASSEMBLY this twenty-sixth day of November, 1949, do HEREBY ADOPT, ENACT AND GIVE TO OURSELVES THIS CONSTITUTION.

42nd Amendment (1976) added Socialist, Secular, and integrity.

**Sovereign.** No external master; Constitution supreme inside India.
**Socialist.** Welfare responsibility and reduction of inequality, not a textbook Marxist state.
**Secular.** No official religion; principled distance plus equal respect; state may reform secular aspects of religious practice.
**Democratic.** Universal adult franchise and responsible executive; also a claim toward social and economic democracy.
**Republic.** Elected head of state.
**Justice.** Social, economic, political — in that order.
**Liberty.** Thought, expression, belief, faith and worship, subject to reasonable restrictions.
**Equality.** Status and opportunity (Articles 14–18), not equality of outcome.
**Fraternity.** Dignity of the individual and unity and integrity of the nation. Ambedkar warned that without fraternity, liberty and equality will not last.

Berubari Union (1960) said the Preamble is not part of the Constitution. Kesavananda Bharati (1973) held that it is. LIC of India (1995) reaffirmed this.

Prelims traps: “federal”, “parliamentary” and “judicial review” are not Preamble words. Dignity of the individual is present through fraternity.

## Basic structure

Article 368 lets Parliament amend the Constitution. It cannot destroy the Constitution’s basic identity. Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973), 13-judge bench, 7–6. Illustrative elements: supremacy of the Constitution, republican and democratic form, secular character, separation of powers, federal character, judicial review, free and fair elections, Article 32, limited amending power, unity and integrity, harmony of Fundamental Rights and Directive Principles, rule of law, independence of judiciary.

Lock one sentence: amendment is a constitutional power, not a power sitting above the Constitution.

# BLOCK C — Physical Geography of India (60 minutes)

Cause and effect: plate motion → Himalaya → monsoon barrier → river regimes → soils → crops → settlement → disaster.

India is in the Northern Hemisphere. Mainland roughly 8°4'N to 37°6'N and 68°7'E to 97°25'E. Tropic of Cancer passes through Gujarat, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, West Bengal, Tripura and Mizoram. IST is 82°30'E near Mirzapur. About two hours of local sun-time difference between Gujarat and Arunachal.

The Peninsula is an old Gondwana fragment. Deccan Traps are flood basalts as India passed over the Reunion hotspot. The Himalaya formed when the Indian plate collided with Eurasia and the Tethys closed. The northern plains are a foreland basin.

Himalaya south to north: Shiwalik (outer, young, duns), Lesser Himalaya / Himachal (Pir Panjal, Dhaula Dhar; hill stations; Kashmir valley between Pir Panjal and Himadri), Great Himalaya / Himadri (highest line; Kanchenjunga highest peak in India), Trans-Himalaya (Zaskar, Ladakh, Kailash, Karakoram; K2 is in Karakoram, not Himadri proper).

West to east: Punjab Himalaya (Indus–Sutlej), Kumaon (Sutlej–Kali), Nepal (Kali–Tista), Assam (Tista–Dihang), then Purvachal bending south. Syntaxial bends at Nanga Parbat and Namcha Barwa.

Plains southward: Bhabar (porous pebbles, streams vanish), Terai (water re-emerges, marshy), Bhangar (old alluvium, kankar), Khadar (new alluvium).

Peninsula: Narmada and Tapti flow west in rift valleys of the Narmada-Son lineament. Western Ghats are higher and more continuous than Eastern Ghats. Anaimudi is the highest peninsular peak. Windward Konkan is wet; leeward Deccan is rain-shadow.

Thar is rain-shadow plus continentality, not merely sand. Western coastal plain is narrow; eastern is wider with large deltas. Andaman-Nicobar sit on a subduction island arc (Barren Island active). Lakshadweep are coral atolls.

Seismicity clusters in the Himalayan belt, the Indo-Burmese arc, Kachchh, and some peninsular faults (Latur 1993).

# BLOCK D — CSAT number sense (40 minutes)

Convert before you compute. 3/8 = 0.375 which is greater than 0.36.
17.5% of 640: 10% = 64, 5% = 32, 2.5% = 16, total 112.

If A:B = 2:3 and B:C = 4:5, A:B:C = 8:12:15.

A number up 20% then down 20% is 1.2 × 0.8 = 0.96, a 4% net fall.
Successive discounts 10% and 20% equal 28% off, not 30%.

Average of 5 numbers is 20 → sum 100. Sixth number 32 → new average 22.

54 km/h = 15 m/s. A 300 m train crosses a pole in 20 s and a 200 m platform in 500/15 = 33.33 s.

Comprehension drill: Ambedkar said political democracy cannot last unless social democracy lies at its base. Claim: votes are not enough. Assumption: social democracy means equality of status in daily life.
