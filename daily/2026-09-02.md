# BLOCK A — What Parliament is (20 minutes)

Article **79**: there shall be a Parliament for the Union, consisting of the **President** and two Houses — the **Council of States (Rajya Sabha)** and the **House of the People (Lok Sabha)**.

Three-part body. The President is not a third chamber that debates, but no Bill becomes an Act without the President’s assent (you already know the assent / return / Money-Bill rules). When a question says “Parliament consists of two Houses only,” it is wrong.

**Why two Houses?**

- Lok Sabha is the **popular** House: people, in territorial constituencies, for a term that can be cut short by dissolution.
- Rajya Sabha is the **federal and continuing** House: States (and specified Union Territories) send members; it cannot be dissolved as a whole.
- The President is the **formal** part of Parliament: summons, addresses, assents, nominates twelve to the Rajya Sabha.

**Where this lives.** Part V, Chapter II, Articles **79 to 122**. Privileges and officers sit in the same chapter. Procedure for financial business sits partly here and partly in the money articles (112–117) you will reuse on the Budget day; tonight you need enough money-procedure to sort a Money Bill from an ordinary Bill.

**Parliament is not the State Legislature.** A State has a Governor + Assembly, and some States also have a Legislative Council. Do not mix Article 168 language into Article 79.

# BLOCK B — Rajya Sabha: composition, election, special powers (45 minutes)

## Strength and mix

Article **80** caps the Council of States at **250**:

- **238** representatives of States and of the Union Territories, allocated as Parliament by law provides, and listed in the **Fourth Schedule**;
- **12** nominated by the President from persons with special knowledge or practical experience in **literature, science, art and social service**.

The working House is smaller than the ceiling because allocation follows the Fourth Schedule as amended when States were reorganised. For Prelims, hold both numbers: **ceiling 250 = 238 + 12**. Current effective elected-plus-nominated strength tracks the Schedule in force on the date of the paper; do not invent a live headcount unless a question prints one.

**Who elects the State representatives?** Elected members of the **Legislative Assembly** of the State, by **proportional representation by means of the single transferable vote**. This is an indirect, Assembly-room election, not a public booth election.

**Union Territories.** Only those UTs that the Constitution and Parliament have brought into the Rajya Sabha scheme send members. Teaching line: **Delhi, Puducherry and Jammu & Kashmir** are the UT names you must be ready to place in the Rajya Sabha story as the law stands after J&K became a Union Territory with a legislature-in-design; allocation still follows the statute and Fourth Schedule as amended. Nominated members of an Assembly do not vote in this election.

**Nominated twelve** sit, speak, vote on ordinary and Constitution-amendment business of the House. They are **not** in the presidential electoral college (Day 6). They **are** in the vice-presidential electoral college (both Houses of Parliament, including nominated members — different college; file that contrast).

## Term and vacancies

- Rajya Sabha is a **permanent House**. It is **not dissolved**.
- As near as possible, **one-third** of members retire every **second year**. A member’s individual term is **six years**.
- A member chosen to fill a casual vacancy sits for the **remainder** of the predecessor’s term.
- There is no constitutional bar on re-election.

**Vice-President** is the **ex-officio Chairman** of the Rajya Sabha (Article 64, and Article 89). A **Deputy Chairman** is elected from among the members. When the Vice-President is acting as President, he does not preside here.

## Special powers of Rajya Sabha (learn as four verbs)

Lok Sabha is stronger on money and on dismissing a government. Rajya Sabha is stronger on **federal alarm bells**.

1. **Article 249.** If Rajya Sabha resolves by **two-thirds of members present and voting** that it is necessary or expedient in the **national interest** that Parliament should legislate on a matter in the **State List**, Parliament may do so. The resolution lasts one year and can be renewed. Laws so made leave the State List intact as a list; they are a temporary Union overlay.
2. **Article 312.** If Rajya Sabha so resolves by the same **two-thirds present and voting** majority, Parliament may create **All-India Services** common to the Union and the States. IAS, IPS, and IFS (Forest) live in this logic. A new All-India Service cannot be invented by Lok Sabha alone.
3. **No dissolution**, so it can sit when Lok Sabha has been dissolved. That is why an ordinance must be laid before *both* Houses when they reassemble — but a dissolved Lok Sabha is not in session, while Rajya Sabha may be.
4. **State voice on federal amendments.** Rajya Sabha cannot be bypassed by a joint sitting on a Constitution Amendment Bill (Day 6). On ordinary Bills, joint sitting exists (below); on 368 Bills, it does not.

**What Rajya Sabha cannot do.** It cannot reject or amend a **Money Bill**. It cannot pass a motion of no-confidence in the Council of Ministers. It cannot force a Finance Bill through against Lok Sabha. Permanence is not primacy on the purse or on the life of the government.

# BLOCK C — Lok Sabha: composition, duration, officers (45 minutes)

## Strength

Article **81**: the House of the People shall consist of

- not more than **530** members chosen by direct election from territorial constituencies in the **States**;
- not more than **20** members to represent the **Union Territories**, chosen in such manner as Parliament by law provides.

The older special nomination of Anglo-Indian members under Article 331 was ended by the **104th Amendment**. Do not write “two nominated Anglo-Indians” as a living feature.

**Ceiling language versus working House.** The Constitution writes maxima. The working strength is fixed by law and by the delimitation order in force. For years the working Lok Sabha has been **543** elected members (States + UTs together under the current allocation). Hold **543** as the working elected House and **550** as the Article 81 ceiling story (530 + 20). If a future delimitation law changes the working number, the ceiling in Article 81 is still the constitutional lid unless 81 itself is amended (and 81’s federal cousins can drag Ladder 3 if representation of States is rewritten — Day 6 logic).

**Direct election.** Adult suffrage. Territorial constituencies. Each State is allotted seats so that the ratio of seats to population is, so far as practicable, the same among States, and inside a State the ratio of population to seats is the same among constituencies — subject to the **small-State floor** and to the **42nd/84th freeze** on readjustment that you must state carefully:

- After the 42nd Amendment, allocation of seats among States was frozen to a specified census so that States which slowed population growth would not lose House strength to States which did not.
- The **84th Amendment** continued the freeze on inter-State allocation and on the total elected strength, while allowing *readjustment of constituency boundaries inside a State* on later census figures.
- The **87th Amendment** tied that intra-State redraw to the 2001 census for the delimitation that produced the present map of constituencies.
- A later delimitation, after the freeze designed around the first census after 2026, is a live political fact. Teaching line for 2027: **the freeze was a federal bargain about numbers; lifting it is a representation fight, not a trivia date.** Write the principle. Do not invent the exact post-freeze seat table until Parliament and the Delimitation Commission print one.

**Reservation of seats.** Article **330** reserves seats in Lok Sabha for **Scheduled Castes** and **Scheduled Tribes** in proportion to their population in each State. The 104th Amendment extended that reservation (and the parallel Assembly reservation under 332) but ended the Anglo-Indian nomination. Reserved constituencies rotate by delimitation; they are not a separate House.

## Duration, sessions, vacancies

- Normal term: **five years** from the first meeting (Article **83**). Not from the election-result day as such — from first sitting.
- During a Proclamation of Emergency, Parliament may extend the term by law for **one year at a time**, and not beyond six months after the Proclamation ceases.
- The President may **dissolve** Lok Sabha earlier. Rajya Sabha has no such death.
- A member’s seat falls vacant on resignation to the Speaker, on disqualification, on absence for **sixty days** without permission (the House may declare the seat vacant), or on death.

**Sessions (Article 85).** The President summons each House from time to time. **Six months** shall not intervene between the last sitting of one session and the first sitting of the next. He may prorogue either House. He may dissolve Lok Sabha.

Learn three words:

- **Adjournment** — House stops for hours or days; the session lives; the Speaker / Chairman does this.
- **Prorogation** — session dies; pending ordinary business generally falls (with important exceptions the Houses’ practice and statute recognise); the President does this.
- **Dissolution** — Lok Sabha itself dies; a general election must follow; only the President, on advice that in a healthy system tracks the PM who still commands or has lost the House.

**Quorum** is **one-tenth** of the total number of members of the House (Article **100**), unless Parliament by law sets another figure. A sitting without quorum is not a valid law-making sitting.

## Officers

- **Speaker** and **Deputy Speaker** are elected by the House from among its members (Article **93**). The Speaker remains Speaker even after dissolution, until the next House meets — so that a referee exists.
- The Speaker certifies **Money Bills** (Article **110(3)** — that certificate is final). The Speaker adjourns, maintains order, decides points of order, and is the channel for several disqualification references under the Tenth Schedule as that Schedule is read with the officers of each House.
- The Speaker **does not vote** in the first instance, but has a **casting vote** on a tie (Article **100**). Same rule for the person in the Chair in Rajya Sabha, except that the Vice-President as Chairman is not a member and therefore has only a casting vote, not an ordinary vote.
- Removal of the Speaker: a resolution of the House passed by a **majority of all the then members**, after **14 days’ notice** (Article **94**). When such a resolution is under consideration, the Speaker does not preside.

**Leader of the House** is a political office (normally the PM if he is in Lok Sabha, or a senior minister). **Leader of the Opposition** is recognised by statute and practice when a party meets the strength convention; it is not Article 79 text.

# BLOCK D — How a Bill becomes an Act; Money Bills; joint sitting (55 minutes)

## Ordinary Bills (Article 107)

An ordinary Bill may start in **either House**. It must be passed by both Houses, with or without amendments agreed between them. Then it goes to the President.

Three readings is parliamentary practice, not a mystical constitutional number: introduction; consideration clause by clause; passing. A Bill pending in Parliament does not lapse on **prorogation**. A Bill pending in the Lok Sabha lapses on **dissolution** of Lok Sabha. A Bill pending in Rajya Sabha which has **not** been passed by Lok Sabha does **not** lapse on dissolution. A Bill passed by Lok Sabha and pending in Rajya Sabha **lapses** on dissolution of Lok Sabha — unless the President has already notified a joint sitting (the saving in Article 108).

## Money Bills (Article 110) — the tight definition

A Bill is a Money Bill if it contains **only** provisions dealing with all or any of:

- imposition, abolition, remission, alteration or regulation of any **tax**;
- borrowing of money by the Government of India, or giving of any guarantee, or amendment of the law on government financial obligations;
- custody of the **Consolidated Fund** or the **Contingency Fund**, payment into or withdrawal from either;
- appropriation of moneys out of the Consolidated Fund;
- declaring any expenditure **charged** on the Consolidated Fund, or increasing the amount of any such charged expenditure;
- receipt of money on account of the Consolidated Fund or the public account, or the audit of the accounts of the Union or of a State; or
- any matter **incidental** to those heads.

A Bill is **not** a Money Bill merely because it provides for the imposition of fines, or for demand of fees for licences or services, or for local taxes by a local authority for local purposes.

**Only Lok Sabha** may introduce a Money Bill. The President’s **prior recommendation** is required for introduction.

After Lok Sabha passes it, Rajya Sabha may **not amend** it. Rajya Sabha may suggest recommendations within **14 days**. Lok Sabha may accept or reject every recommendation. If Rajya Sabha does nothing in 14 days, the Bill is deemed passed by both Houses in the form Lok Sabha passed it.

The **Speaker’s certificate** that a Bill is a Money Bill is **final**. Courts have historically been slow to second-guess that certificate; later constitutional conversation about whether a mixed finance statute was wrongly tagged is a Mains nuance, not a licence to treat 110 as optional.

**Money Bill ≠ Finance Bill in casual speech.** A Finance Bill accompanying the Budget often contains tax clauses plus other clauses. If it contains *only* 110 matters, it is a Money Bill. If it contains other matters too, it is a financial Bill of a wider kind and Rajya Sabha’s ordinary legislative role revives. Sort the text, do not sort the nickname.

**Charged expenditure** (examples you should name): emoluments of the President; salaries of the Chairman and Deputy Chairman of Rajya Sabha and of the Speaker and Deputy Speaker; salaries, allowances and pensions of Supreme Court judges; salary of the CAG; debt charges of the Government of India; certain court and pension items the text lists. Charged items are **discussed but not voted** in the same way as voted grants. That is how the independence of some offices is paid for.

## Financial Bills that are not Money Bills

A Bill that involves expenditure from the Consolidated Fund, or a tax-plus-other-clauses Bill, still needs the President’s recommendation at the prescribed stage, but Rajya Sabha can amend it. Joint sitting can apply. Do not call every Budget-week Bill a Money Bill.

## Joint sitting (Article 108)

If a Bill (not a Money Bill, not a Constitution Amendment Bill) has been passed by one House and:

- rejected by the other, or
- the Houses have finally disagreed on amendments, or
- more than **six months** have elapsed from the date of reception in the other House without it being passed,

the President **may** notify a joint sitting, unless the Bill has already lapsed by dissolution in a way 108 does not save.

At the joint sitting, the Bill is passed by a **majority of the total number of members of both Houses present and voting**. Lok Sabha’s larger size is the arithmetic advantage. The Speaker of Lok Sabha presides; if not, a person appointed by the President (the Deputy Speaker or another agreed person in the living practice).

Joint sittings have been rare. Rarity is not absence from the syllabus.

## Disagreement map (memorise as a table in your head)

| Kind of Bill | Joint sitting? | Rajya Sabha can amend? | Special note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ordinary | Yes, if 108 conditions | Yes | Either House may originate |
| Money Bill (110) | No | No; 14-day recommend only | Only Lok Sabha originates |
| Constitution Amendment (368) | No | Yes; must pass separately | Special majority each House; maybe State ratification |

# BLOCK E — Membership: qualifications, disqualifications, privileges (40 minutes)

## Qualifications (Articles 84, 102 and the statute)

For **both Houses**, a member must be a **citizen of India**, make the prescribed oath or affirmation (Third Schedule), and meet the age floor:

- Lok Sabha: **25 years**;
- Rajya Sabha: **30 years**.

Parliament by law adds other qualifications. The Representation of the People Act requires that a Rajya Sabha candidate be an **elector in any parliamentary constituency** in India (the old “elector in that State only” rule was dropped by statute). A Lok Sabha candidate must be an elector in any constituency, and additional rules apply for reserved seats (the candidate must belong to the SC or ST for whom the seat is reserved; an ST seat in a specified tribal area follows the Act).

## Disqualifications (Article 102 + Tenth Schedule + statute)

A person is disqualified for being chosen as, and for being, a member if he

- holds an **office of profit** under the Government of India or a State Government, other than an office exempted by Parliament by law;
- is of unsound mind and so declared by a competent court;
- is an undischarged **insolvent**;
- is not a citizen, or has voluntarily acquired citizenship of a foreign State, or is under acknowledgment of allegiance to a foreign State;
- is disqualified by any law made by Parliament (the Representation of the People Act: corrupt practice, certain convictions with a sentence threshold, government-contract interest in specified form, office of profit details, and so on).

**Decision on 102 disputes** (except the Tenth Schedule) is by the **President** after obtaining the **opinion of the Election Commission**; that opinion is binding in this procedure (Article **103**). Parallel Article 192 at State level uses the Governor + EC.

**Tenth Schedule (anti-defection).** A member is disqualified if he voluntarily gives up his political party’s membership, or votes (or abstains) contrary to the party whip in the House unless condoned within the permitted days. An independent member is disqualified if he joins a party after election. A nominated member is disqualified if he joins a party after six months. Merger of parties has a numerical safe-harbour (two-thirds of the legislature party). The **Speaker / Chairman** decides; the office-of-Speaker-as-tribunal story and the scope of judicial review are Mains-grade: review exists for perversity and constitutional error; the House officer is not a king.

**Office of profit** is an exam evergreen. The idea: you should not be on the government payroll in a way that makes you a paid servant of the executive while you are supposed to watch that executive. Parliament has listed exempted offices. A ministership is constitutionally compatible because Article 75 / 164 *expect* ministers to be legislators (with a six-month window to get a seat).

## Oath, salaries, privileges

Every member, before taking a seat, makes the Third Schedule oath or affirmation before a person appointed by the President (Article **99**).

Article **105** privileges:

- freedom of **speech in Parliament** (subject to the Constitution and the rules of procedure);
- no court proceeding for a vote or speech in Parliament or a committee;
- other privileges that at the commencement of the Constitution were those of the House of Commons, and now such privileges as Parliament defines by law, and until so defined, those they had immediately before the 44th Amendment restated the clause.

The 44th Amendment took the express “House of Commons” comparison out of the living text for future growth, but the working body of privilege (publication of proceedings, internal discipline, bar on court inquiry into internal procedure under Article **122**) remains. Article 122: courts shall not inquire into the validity of parliamentary proceedings on the ground of alleged irregularity of procedure. That is not a shield for a law that violates a Fundamental Right; it is a shield for *how the House ran its debate*.

**Parliamentary privilege versus Article 19 and 21** is an ethics-and-law edge: the House may punish contempt; it may not become a small criminal court that ignores the rest of the Constitution.

# BLOCK F — Devices that make the House a watchdog (25 minutes)

These are rules-of-procedure creatures more than Article-number creatures. Prelims still asks them because they are how accountability looks on a sitting day.

- **Question Hour** — first hour on a normal sitting. Starred questions get oral answers and supplementaries; unstarred get written answers; short-notice questions need the Speaker’s leave and a minister willing to answer on short time.
- **Zero Hour** — Indian practice, not a Westminster name in Erskine May. Members raise matters that cannot wait for a notice cycle. No guaranteed relief; it is oxygen, not a statute.
- **Adjournment motion** (Lok Sabha) — to adjourn the listed business and discuss a definite matter of urgent public importance. Rarely admitted; admission is itself a political shock.
- **Calling attention, short-duration discussion, half-hour discussion** — graded ways to force a minister onto the floor without toppling the government.
- **No-confidence motion** — only in **Lok Sabha**, against the Council of Ministers as a whole. If carried, the Council must resign. Rajya Sabha may debate a government, not eject it.
- **Censure / privilege / privilege committee** — targeted blame, not always a fall of government.
- **Budget devices** — general discussion, scrutiny by the Departmentally Related Standing Committees, cut motions (policy cut, economy cut, token cut) on demand for grants, then the Appropriation Bill and the Finance Bill. Cut motions are Lok Sabha weapons.
- **Committees** you must name: Public Accounts Committee (examines CAG reports; conventionally chaired by an Opposition member); Estimates Committee; Committee on Public Undertakings; Departmentally Related Standing Committees (both Houses); Business Advisory Committee; Privileges; Petitions; Ethics in the living practice of each House.

A House that only passes the government’s calendar and never uses these devices is a stamp, not a Parliament.

# BLOCK G — Map lesson: States, Union Territories, neighbours (75 minutes)

Close your eyes and build India from the outside in. First the land neighbours, then the sea neighbours, then the State chain, then the anomalous lines (chicken’s neck, tri-junctions, a coastline State that does not look coastal on a cheap map).

## Land neighbours (seven)

Clockwise from the north-west:

1. **Pakistan** — borders **Gujarat, Rajasthan, Punjab, and the Union Territory of Jammu & Kashmir** (and the Line of Control geometry that Prelims will often name as LoC, not as an ordinary district boundary). The Radcliffe line in the plains; the Sir Creek argument on the Gujarat–Sindh marsh; the desert border in Rajasthan.
2. **Afghanistan** — a short theoretical touch in the high north-west of the old princely J&K story (Wakhan corridor). In the living political map this is the **Durand / Wakhan** conversation; many exam keys still print Afghanistan as a neighbour. Write it as a **short north-west land neighbour** via the J&K / PoK claimed-boundary frame the question is using. If a paper uses “India’s current administered land border,” read the question twice.
3. **China** — **Ladakh UT, Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Sikkim, Arunachal Pradesh**. The Line of Actual Control is not a settled international boundary. Aksai Chin and the eastern sector (McMahon Line / Arunachal) are the two classic sectors; the middle sector is the quieter Himachal–Uttarakhand belt. Sikkim’s join in 1975 made the Chumbi Valley a strategic wedge between Sikkim and Bhutan.
4. **Nepal** — **Uttarakhand, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, West Bengal, Sikkim**. Open border in the social sense; not a lawless border in the constitutional sense. The Kali / Lipulekh / Limpiyadhura map dispute sits at the Uttarakhand end. The Siliguri corridor feels Nepal and Bangladesh on two shoulders.
5. **Bhutan** — **Sikkim, West Bengal, Assam, Arunachal Pradesh**. No open-border myth of the Nepal kind; a treaty relationship and a very watched Doklam plateau near the Chumbi Valley.
6. **Myanmar** — **Arunachal Pradesh, Nagaland, Manipur, Mizoram**. Hills, unfenced stretches, the Free Movement Regime debate, and the Kaladan project’s logic all live here. Myanmar is also a **Bay of Bengal** sea neighbour in a wider sense, but the land four-State touch is the map fact.
7. **Bangladesh** — **West Bengal, Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, Mizoram**. The longest land border. The 2015 Land Boundary Agreement and the exchange of enclaves (the old Dahagram–Angarpota / Tin Bigha story) is the settled teaching end of a very old messy line. Sundarbans is a shared mangrove, not only a West Bengal park.

**Not a land neighbour:** Sri Lanka (sea), Maldives (sea), Indonesia (sea, far Andaman), Thailand (sea near Andaman), Oman (sea, far west). **Bhutan is landlocked** relative to the ocean; Nepal is landlocked. China is not an Indian Ocean coastal neighbour of India.

## Sea neighbours and water facts

- **Sri Lanka** — across the **Palk Strait** and the **Gulf of Mannar**. **Adam’s Bridge / Ram Setu** is the shoal chain. The International Maritime Boundary Line and the fishermen’s livelihood quarrel are one file. The nearest Indian State is **Tamil Nadu**; the nearest UT story is the Gulf of Mannar biosphere, not a second country.
- **Maldives** — south-west, beyond **Lakshadweep**’s waters. Eight-degree and nine-degree channels are Indian water-gates: **Eight Degree Channel** separates Maldives from Minicoy; **Nine Degree Channel** separates Minicoy from the rest of Lakshadweep. **Ten Degree Channel** separates Andaman from Nicobar.
- **Indonesia** — across the **Indira Point** waters of Great Nicobar (India’s southernmost village point; the southernmost *territory* fact you should not confuse with Kanyakumari, which is the southernmost point of the peninsula mainland).
- **Thailand and Myanmar** also face the Andaman Sea.
- **Pakistan** is a land *and* Arabian Sea neighbour (Gujarat coast / Sir Creek).
- **Bangladesh** is a land *and* Bay of Bengal neighbour.

**Mainland extremes (conventional teaching points):**

- North: **Indira Col** area in the Siachen / Karakoram frame (questions sometimes still say *Kashmir* generally).
- South (mainland): **Cape Comorin / Kanyakumari** (Tamil Nadu).
- South (territory): **Indira Point**, Great Nicobar.
- West: **Ghuar Mota** (Gujarat) is a common textbook west-point name; **Dwarka–Kutch** belt is the western wall.
- East: **Kibithu** (Arunachal Pradesh).

**Standard time.** Indian Standard Time is **82°30′ E**, near **Mirzapur (Uttar Pradesh)** / the Allahabad–Mirzapur belt. That meridian is also a rough east–west divider of the country’s longitude span.

## The 28 States, as a memory walk, not an alphabet dump

Walk **west to east along the Himalaya**, then **down the Gangetic plain**, then **the north-east**, then **the peninsula clockwise from Gujarat**, then **the two island UTs and the city UTs**.

### Himalayan and western mountain wall

- **Jammu & Kashmir (UT)** and **Ladakh (UT)** — after the 2019 reorganisation of the former State. J&K is designed with a legislature; Ladakh without one. Both touch China; J&K also faces the Pakistan line.
- **Himachal Pradesh** — China (LAC) in the east-north; Punjab, Haryana, Uttarakhand, J&K / Ladakh around it. Shimla is the summer-old capital story; the Satluj cuts through.
- **Uttarakhand** — China and Nepal; Uttar Pradesh to the south. Two divisions in ordinary speech: Garhwal and Kumaon. Gangotri–Yamunotri source region.
- **Sikkim** — China, Nepal, Bhutan; West Bengal to the south. Smallest mainland State by population in many tables; Kanchenjunga on the Nepal–Sikkim wall.
- **Arunachal Pradesh** — China, Myanmar, Bhutan; Assam to the south. Largest State in the north-east by area. McMahon Line debate lives here.

### The great plain and the desert

- **Punjab** — Pakistan; Rajasthan, Haryana, Himachal; Chandigarh UT as shared capital with Haryana.
- **Haryana** — wraps Delhi on three sides; no international border.
- **Rajasthan** — Pakistan; largest State by area. Thar. Touches Punjab, Haryana, UP, MP, Gujarat.
- **Gujarat** — Pakistan (Sir Creek / Kutch); Arabian Sea; Rajasthan, MP, Maharashtra, and a Dadra & Nagar Haveli and Daman & Diu UT jigsaw on its coast and interior pockets.
- **Uttar Pradesh** — Nepal; largest State by population. Touches Uttarakhand, Himachal (a sliver conversation via the north-west), Haryana, Delhi, Rajasthan, MP, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, Bihar. Do not miss **Chhattisgarh** and **Jharkhand** on UP’s southern-eastern rim.
- **Bihar** — Nepal; UP, Jharkhand, West Bengal.
- **West Bengal** — Nepal, Bhutan, Bangladesh; the **Siliguri corridor (chicken’s neck)** is the thin land that holds the north-east to the rest of India. Odisha, Jharkhand, Bihar, Sikkim, Assam (via the corridor).

### The north-east seven (plus Sikkim as the eighth sister in political speech)

A clean mnemonic walk from the corridor:

- **Assam** — Bangladesh; the Brahmaputra State; touches Arunachal, Nagaland, Manipur, Mizoram, Tripura, Meghalaya, West Bengal. Capital region: Dispur (Guwahati).
- **Meghalaya** — Bangladesh; created from Assam; Shillong plateau; wet south slope (you already know Mawsynram).
- **Tripura** — almost wrapped by Bangladesh; Assam and Mizoram on the Indian side.
- **Mizoram** — Bangladesh and Myanmar; Assam, Tripura, Manipur.
- **Manipur** — Myanmar; Nagaland, Assam, Mizoram. Imphal valley versus surrounding hills is an internal geography you will need for Internal Security later.
- **Nagaland** — Myanmar; Assam, Arunachal, Manipur.
- **Arunachal** — already placed on the China wall.

**Which north-eastern States have no international border?** None of the Seven Sisters is landlocked *inside India only* except that **Meghalaya, Assam, Tripura, Mizoram, Manipur, Nagaland, Arunachal** all touch a foreign country except — wait, check: **Assam touches Bangladesh**. **Meghalaya–Bangladesh**. All seven except? **Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, Mizoram, Manipur, Nagaland, Arunachal** — all seven have a foreign border. **Sikkim** too. The State in the north-east political family that is easy to mis-label is **Assam**: people forget the Bangladesh touch in the west and south-west. **Nagaland** does not touch Bangladesh. **Manipur** does not touch Bangladesh. There is **no** Sister without a foreign border.

Correction the examiner wants: every one of the Seven Sisters has an international border. (Assam–Bangladesh; Meghalaya–Bangladesh; Tripura–Bangladesh; Mizoram–Bangladesh and Myanmar; Manipur–Myanmar; Nagaland–Myanmar; Arunachal–China, Myanmar, Bhutan.)

### Central and eastern peninsula

- **Madhya Pradesh** — no international border; large; touches UP, Rajasthan, Gujarat, Maharashtra, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand? MP touches UP, Rajasthan, Gujarat, Maharashtra, Chhattisgarh. After the 2000 bifurcation, **Chhattisgarh** took the east. MP does **not** touch Jharkhand in the usual present map (Chhattisgarh and UP/Bihar sit in between depending on the corner — the living map: MP–Jharkhand is **not** a border; Chhattisgarh and UP separate them).
- **Chhattisgarh** — UP, MP, Maharashtra, Telangana, Odisha, Jharkhand, Bihar (a short northern touch in some atlases — the **Bihar–Chhattisgarh** contact is easy to miss or to over-claim; the safe teaching set is UP, MP, Maharashtra, Jharkhand, Odisha, Telangana. Confirm Bihar as *not* a default neighbour in many school maps: Bihar’s south is Jharkhand. Do **not** list Bihar as a Chhattisgarh neighbour.)
- **Jharkhand** — Bihar, UP, Chhattisgarh, Odisha, West Bengal. No international border. Plateau and coal.
- **Odisha** — Bay of Bengal; West Bengal, Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, Andhra Pradesh. No Bangladesh touch (West Bengal sits north).

### Western and southern peninsula

- **Maharashtra** — Arabian Sea; Gujarat, MP, Chhattisgarh, Telangana, Karnataka, Goa, and the UT of Dadra & Nagar Haveli and Daman & Diu on the northern coastal story. No international land border.
- **Goa** — smallest State by area; Maharashtra and Karnataka; Arabian Sea.
- **Karnataka** — Arabian Sea; Goa, Maharashtra, Telangana, Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu, Kerala. No international land border.
- **Telangana** — landlocked; Maharashtra, Chhattisgarh, Odisha? **No Odisha.** Telangana touches Maharashtra, Chhattisgarh, Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka. Created in 2014 from Andhra Pradesh.
- **Andhra Pradesh** — Bay of Bengal; Odisha, Chhattisgarh? After 2014, AP touches Odisha, Telangana, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu. **Chhattisgarh–AP** is **not** a border; Telangana sits between most of that former line. A tiny debate exists at corners — teaching line for GS: **AP neighbours = Odisha, Telangana, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu**, plus the Bay.
- **Tamil Nadu** — Sri Lanka across the strait; Kerala, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh; Bay of Bengal and Indian Ocean at Kanyakumari.
- **Kerala** — Arabian Sea; Karnataka and Tamil Nadu. No international land border.

### Union Territories you must place

1. **Jammu & Kashmir** — legislature designed; Srinagar / Jammu seasonal capitals in ordinary speech.
2. **Ladakh** — Leh–Kargil; no legislature; China and the high passes.
3. **Delhi (NCT)** — a legislature and a special Article 239AA design; landlocked inside Haryana and UP.
4. **Chandigarh** — capital of both Punjab and Haryana; itself a UT; no legislature.
5. **Puducherry** — four **unconnected** bits: Puducherry and Karaikal (Tamil Nadu coast), Yanam (Andhra coast), Mahe (Kerala coast). A legislature. Former French pockets.
6. **Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu** — merged UT (2020). Daman and Diu are coastal Gujarat-adjacent; Dadra & Nagar Haveli is an inland pocket. No legislature of the Delhi kind.
7. **Lakshadweep** — Arabian Sea; atolls; closest mainland is Kerala.
8. **Andaman and Nicobar Islands** — Bay of Bengal / Andaman Sea; Port Blair; Indira Point; Ten Degree Channel between Andaman and Nicobar groups.

- **Shimla** / Dharamshala winter-summer talk in Himachal is practice; Shimla is the printed capital.
- **Srinagar and Jammu** are the living seasonal pair for J&K.

- **Pakistan:** Gujarat, Rajasthan, Punjab, J&K UT. (Not Haryana, not Himachal.)
- **China:** Ladakh, Himachal, Uttarakhand, Sikkim, Arunachal. (Not Assam, not Nagaland, not J&K-as-administered if the paper separates Ladakh — after 2019 the China land touch of the old J&K State is largely the **Ladakh** UT sector plus the LoC/LAC complexity; J&K UT’s main international face in many maps is Pakistan / LoC.)
- **Nepal:** Uttarakhand, UP, Bihar, West Bengal, Sikkim. (Not Himachal, not Jharkhand.)

- **Myanmar:** Arunachal, Nagaland, Manipur, Mizoram. (Not Assam, not Tripura.)

## The Siliguri corridor

A thin belt of **West Bengal** between Nepal and Bangladesh. All road, rail and military movement to the north-east that does not fly or go through another country is squeezed here. Sikkim hangs north of it. This is why a Bhutan–China scrape at Doklam is an Indian strategic fact, not only a Bhutanese one.

# BLOCK H — Paired block: economy, current affairs explained, ethics (25 minutes)

## Economy

Parliament’s money procedure *is* the economy’s legal gate. No tax without law. No withdrawal from the Consolidated Fund without an Appropriation Act. A government that rules by ordinance on a tax and hopes to tag the statute as a Money Bill to shrink Rajya Sabha is playing with Article 110’s word **only**.

On the map:

- The **Siliguri corridor** is a logistics price on every north-eastern commodity.
- Landlocked **Telangana** and **Chhattisgarh** live on rail-road connectors and on neighbouring ports (Visakhapatnam, Paradip, Mumbai) they do not own.
- **Gujarat–Rajasthan–Punjab** carry the Pakistan-facing trade-and-security premium.
- **Tamil Nadu**’s fishery economy is a Palk Strait economy.
- **Andaman** is a blue-economy and maritime-security economy before it is a tourism poster.
- Delimitation after the population freeze lifts will redistribute not only MPs but **Finance Commission political weight** in the long run, because voice in the Lok Sabha is part of how States bargain.

## Current affairs explained, tied to today

When a Bill is called a Money Bill in the news, open Article 110 and ask whether the clauses are *only* the listed money heads. A tagging fight is a federal fight: Rajya Sabha is the House being asked to sit down.

When Rajya Sabha passes a **249** resolution, Parliament is not rewriting the Seventh Schedule forever; it is renting a State-List field for a year in the national interest. Say that sentence before you praise or damn the topic (internal security, wildlife, public health).

When a delimitation or “north versus south seats” debate returns, you already have the freeze story (42nd / 84th / 87th) and Day 6’s Ladder 3: **representation of States in Parliament** is a ratification subject if the constitutional formula itself is rewritten.

When a Speaker’s Tenth Schedule ruling is in the news, the static hook is: whip + voluntary giving up + merger two-thirds + the Speaker as the first judge + judicial review for perversity. Do not write a party pamphlet.

When a border or a Free Movement Regime with Myanmar, or a Bangladesh fence segment, or a Nepal map row at Lipulekh, hits the paper, start with **which Indian States actually touch that country**. Wrong neighbours lose the mark before the diplomacy starts.

When Parliament is adjourned sine die and then prorogued, remember: adjournment is the Chair; prorogation is the President; pending Bills do not die merely because the session was adjourned.

## Ethics

A Speaker who uses the Tenth Schedule as a party whip from the Chair has broken the referee’s virtue. A majority that certifies a mixed social-policy statute as a Money Bill to gag the Council of States has broken federal good faith even if the certificate is hard to assail in court.

A member who treats Question Hour as theatre and never files a starred question on a constituency famine is using the salary and not the office.

An officer in a border district who speaks of “the other side” as a cartoon, not as a State with a name and a treaty file, will mis-handle the first crisis. Map literacy is an ethical duty because civilians live on the line.

A citizen who wants Rajya Sabha abolished because it “blocks development” has not asked what Article 249 and Article 312 are for. A citizen who wants Lok Sabha never to dissolve has not asked what confidence means.

# BLOCK I — CSAT: permutations, sets, percentages, a map table (40 minutes)

Work on paper first.

### Q1. Seats and ratios

A State has 4.2 crore people and 14 Lok Sabha seats. Average population per seat?

**Solution.** $4.2 \times 10^7 / 14 = 0.3 \times 10^7 = $ **30 lakh** per seat.

### Q2. Special majority arithmetic (revision from Day 6, now in a House)

Rajya Sabha working strength tonight is 245. A 249 resolution needs two-thirds of members **present and voting**. If 180 members vote, minimum yes votes?

**Solution.** Two-thirds of 180 = 120. So **120** yes votes. (249 does *not* use the 368 double test of total membership.)

### Q3. Money Bill timeline

Lok Sabha passes a Money Bill on 1 June and transmits it the same day. Last day for Rajya Sabha to return recommendations?

**Solution.** **14 days** — 15 June if you count 14 clear days from 1 June in the ordinary calendar way used in teaching problems (1 June + 14 days = 15 June). If the House sits on the 15th, that sitting is still inside the window in the usual exam keying of “within 14 days.” Hold the rule as **fourteen days from receipt**, not “two weeks roughly.”

### Q4. Arrangement

Five States sit in a row facing north: Punjab, Haryana, Rajasthan, Gujarat, Maharashtra — but not in that order. Punjab is at one end. Maharashtra is at the other end. Haryana is immediately east of Punjab. Who is in the middle?

**Solution.** Ends: Punjab and Maharashtra. Haryana immediately east of Punjab means the row from west to east starts **Punjab – Haryana – ? – ? – Maharashtra**. The remaining States are Rajasthan and Gujarat. Middle is the third slot: either Rajasthan or Gujarat — **insufficient data** if no further clue.

Add the extra clue printed below the question in a real paper: “Gujarat is next to Maharashtra.” Then west to east: Punjab, Haryana, Rajasthan, Gujarat, Maharashtra. **Middle = Rajasthan.**

### Q5. Sets

In a batch of 60 trainees: 28 have served in a border State, 30 have served in a coastal State, 10 have served in both. How many have served in neither?

**Solution.** Union = $28 + 30 - 10 = 48$. Neither = $60 - 48 = $ **12**.

### Q6. Permutations

Three distinct Bills must be introduced on three distinct days, Monday–Wednesday, one per day. Number of orders?

**Solution.** $3! = $ **6**.

### Q7. Calendar revision

2 September 2026 is Wednesday. What day is 2 December 2026?

**Solution.** Sep 28 days left after 2nd (30–2=28) + Oct 31 + Nov 30 + 2 Dec. Cleaner: from 2 Sep to 2 Dec = 30 days of remaining-September-wait.

From **2 September to 2 October** = 30 days = 4 weeks + 2 odd days.  
2 Oct to 2 Nov = 31 days = 3 odd days.  
2 Nov to 2 Dec = 30 days = 2 odd days.  
Total odd days = 2+3+2 = 7 = 0. So **Wednesday**.

### Q8. Table: neighbours

| State | Sea | Pakistan | China | Nepal | Bhutan | Myanmar | Bangladesh |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P | Yes | Yes | No | No | No | No | No |
| Q | No | No | Yes | Yes | No | No | No |
| R | No | No | Yes | No | Yes | Yes | No |
| S | Yes | No | No | No | No | No | Yes |

Identify:

- P is **Gujarat** (sea + Pakistan; no China).
- Q is **Uttarakhand** (China + Nepal; no sea).
- R is **Arunachal Pradesh** (China, Bhutan, Myanmar).
- S is **West Bengal** (sea + Bangladesh). Could S be Odisha? Odisha has sea but **no** Bangladesh. So S is West Bengal.

### Q9. Percentage

A UT’s elected Lok Sabha seats rise from 1 to 2. Percentage increase?

**Solution.** 100 percent.

### Q10. Statement–conclusion

Statement: “A Money Bill cannot be introduced in the Rajya Sabha.”

(I) Rajya Sabha has no role at all in the passage of a Money Bill.  
(II) Introduction and the final say on recommendations belong to the Lok Sabha.

**Answer:** Only II follows. Rajya Sabha may recommend within 14 days; that is a role, not a veto.
