# BLOCK A — Why Bengal was cut, and what the cut actually did (50 minutes)

## 1. Bengal in 1905 was not “a district that grew too big”

The Bengal Presidency that Curzon faced was a giant administrative pile: Bengal proper, Bihar, and Orissa under one Lieutenant-Governor, with Assam recently attached and detached in earlier experiments. Population was on the order of 78–80 million. Officials said the file-load was impossible. That sentence is true as administration. It is not the whole motive.

**Teaching sentence:** partition was sold as **efficiency**; it was also **a political map drawn across a talking public**.

## 2. The map you must be able to draw

From 16 October 1905:

**Eastern Bengal and Assam** (new province, capital Dacca / Dhaka)
- Eastern Bengal districts (Dacca, Mymensingh, Chittagong, Rajshahi belt, and the rest of the east)
- Assam
- Muslim-majority in the new unit as a whole
- Jute hinterland of the delta; river-and-rail face toward Chittagong

**Bengal** (the reduced province, capital still Calcutta)
- Western Bengal
- Bihar
- Orissa
- Hindu-majority as a whole once the east was peeled off
- Calcutta remained the imperial capital until 1911, but its *political hinterland* was sliced

Do not write “Hindu Bengal versus Muslim Bengal” as if every village flipped religion at the new line. The communal arithmetic was provincial, not household. Peasants on both sides still grew jute and rice. The political class in Calcutta felt the cut as amputation of *their* province and *their* newspaper public.

## 3. Curzon’s other weather — why 1905 felt like a wall

Place partition inside a short Curzon list so Prelims options do not float:

- Delhi Durbar 1903 — imperial spectacle, Victoria dead, Edward VII proclaimed in Indian ceremony.
- Calcutta Corporation trimmed of much of its elected Indian element.
- Indian Universities Act 1904 — tighter official control over syndicates and affiliations; graduates read it as a muzzle on the talking class.
- Frontier and Tibet forward policy charged, as Moderates had always complained, to Indian revenues.
- Then partition, announced 1905 after earlier official notes, executed 16 October 1905.

**Teaching sentence:** Curzon did not invent dislike of educated Indians; he **governed as if their associations were a nuisance to be redesigned.**

## 4. Official reasons versus nationalist reading

**Official file (learn it so you can steelman):**
- Lieutenant-Governor cannot tour and decide for 80 million.
- Assam needs a port and a larger revenue base; Chittagong can serve that.
- Eastern districts are neglected from Calcutta.
- Better police and flood-and-jute administration if the east has its own secretariat.

**Nationalist file (the one that created a movement):**
- “Divide and rule”: peel Muslim-majority east from the Calcutta Hindu-professional public so neither can speak as “Bengal.”
- Punish the loudest nationalist press-city.
- Create a grateful new Muslim elite around Dacca who might treat the Raj as protector against Calcutta domination.

Exam posture: **write both columns**. Then add: even if efficiency was real, the *timing, secrecy of early notes, and refusal to consult the named public* made the efficiency story unbelievable to that public.

## 5. Who opposed, who welcomed

- Calcutta bar, press, students, and much of the professional bhadralok: total opposition. Surendranath Banerjea’s generation and the younger men shared this, even when they later split on method.
- Many eastern Muslim zamindars and professionals: a new province meant jobs, a capital, and an escape from Calcutta’s cultural weight. Nawab Salimullah of Dacca is the name that will return when the League is founded.
- Peasants: not a single voice. Jute prices and rents moved them more than the name of the secretariat.
- British commercial houses on the Hooghly: uneasy about unrest, not about the map as such.

**Teaching sentence:** partition created **two political gifts at once** — a united Hindu-professional rage in the west, and a provincial Muslim opportunity in the east. Indian politics after 1906 lives inside that double gift.

# BLOCK B — Swadeshi and Boycott as a new political technology (55 minutes)

## 1. Two words that are not synonyms

**Boycott:** refuse British cloth, salt, sugar where possible; refuse honorary offices and sometimes government schools. It is a *negative* weapon.

**Swadeshi:** use, make, and honour Indian cloth, soap, matches, banks, insurance, national schools. It is a *positive* weapon.

Together they answer yesterday’s Moderate limit: speech to Westminster had not stopped a Viceroy. Goods and schools might.

**16 October 1905** was observed as a day of mourning in Calcutta: rakhi-tying across communities as a symbol of unity, baths in the Ganga, closed shops. Ritual entered politics. Remember that image; Mains answers need one concrete scene.

## 2. Four strands inside one movement — do not flatten them

Name the strands. Questions love mixing them.

**1. Moderate-constitutional Swadeshi.** Banerjea, the older Congress core: petition *plus* boycott of British goods as pressure, still loyal in language, still hoping London will overrule Curzon.

**2. Constructive Swadeshi.** National education (Bengal National College; later Dawn Society atmosphere; technical and literary self-help), swadeshi stores, insurance and banks, handloom revival. Aurobindo’s early journalism and Tagore’s early hope sit near this strand before Tagore’s later doubts.

**3. Political Extremism.** Tilak in Bombay-Poona, Bipin Chandra Pal and Aurobindo in Bengal, Lala Lajpat Rai in Punjab — the **Lal-Bal-Pal** triangle. Demand rises from “repeal partition” toward *swaraj* as the explicit goal. Method: boycott, passive resistance talk, mass meetings, use of festivals and vernacular.

**4. Revolutionary underground.** Secret samitis, arms, the later Muzaffarpur and Alipore cases — *preview only*. Do not dump 1908–1912 onto the first winter of Swadeshi. Mark that boycott’s street energy and the samiti habit could feed each other.

**Teaching sentence:** Swadeshi is a **coalition of methods sharing one insult**, not a single party line.

## 3. Lal–Bal–Pal — what each actually stood for

**Bal Gangadhar Tilak (Poona / Bombay Presidency).**
- Already famous for Ganapati and Shivaji festivals as public classrooms, and for the famine-and-plague years’ confrontation with authority.
- *Kesari* (Marathi) and *Mahratta* (English): the vernacular is the mass tool.
- Doctrine in one line: *swaraj is my birthright* is the later compact sentence; in this phase the content is — self-rule will not be gifted by speeches in English to a Viceroy who is not listening.
- Social base: Deccan urban and small-town Hindu public; not yet a peasant union in the later Congress sense.

**Bipin Chandra Pal (Bengal).**
- Orator of boycott and national education.
- Stressed that political freedom needed economic self-reliance and a new mind, not only a new council seat.

**Lala Lajpat Rai (Punjab).**
- Arya Samaj schooling background (you already have Dayanand’s world).
- Punjab was a recruiting and canal colony province; his Extremism carried a Punjabi professional and trading public into the all-India quarrel.
- Deported 1907 under the old regulation habit of the Raj — a fact Prelims likes.

**Aurobindo Ghose.**
- Returned from England, ICS-quit story, Baroda service, then Bengal journalism (*Bande Mataram*).
- Gave Extremism a theory of passive resistance and a spiritual-national language. Later revolutionary-case and Pondicherry are next files. Today: he is the thinker who told Bengal that boycott was a moral idea, not only a shopping idea.

**Teaching sentence:** Extremists did not invent hatred of the Raj; they **changed the addressee** — from British liberal opinion to the Indian public itself.

## 4. What Swadeshi built that outlived 1905

- Indian mill demand jumped where consumers actually switched. Bombay and Ahmedabad cloth gained a political market. Matches, soap, and national stores were uneven and often short-lived; say so.
- National education: Bengal National College (Aurobindo as early principal is a standard peg), schools that taught through Indian languages and Indian history. Many collapsed when jobs still required government certificates. The *idea* of an alternative classroom survived.
- A new repertoire: picketing, public burning of foreign cloth, student strikes, rakhi and *Bande Mataram* as political sound.
- Women in urban Bengal entered street and home-based swadeshi work more visibly than in the 1885 Congress hall. Do not exaggerate a mass feminist organisation; do not erase the shift.

## 5. Limits and costs — write them in the same breath

- Boycott hurt jute-belt peasants when Calcutta firms and export chains wobbled; a poor household cannot always pay a swadeshi premium.
- Movement was Bengal-strong, with Bombay-Poona and Punjab echoes; Madras and the United Provinces were warmer to Moderate caution.
- Hindu revivalist colour in festivals and *Bande Mataram* politics frightened some Muslim professionals who had just been offered a new province.
- The Raj answered with police, sedition law, school circulars against student politics, and prosecution of editors.
- Constructive work needed capital and skill; speeches created more stores than factories.

**Teaching sentence:** Swadeshi proved Indians could **punish a government without a sepoy army**; it did not yet prove they could **replace** that government.

# BLOCK C — Surat Split 1907 and the League 1906 (50 minutes)

## 1. The road from Calcutta 1906 to Surat 1907

**Congress, Calcutta, 1906.**
- President: Dadabhai Naoroji (third time).
- The session is the hinge: Extremists push *swaraj*, boycott, swadeshi, national education onto the resolution slate. Moderates accept a careful wording because Naoroji can hold both rooms and because partition is still raw.
- Four words now sit inside Congress language: **Swaraj, Swadeshi, Boycott, National Education.** Remember the four. They are the Extremist minimum.

**1907, Surat.**
- Fight over the presidential chair: Moderates want Rash Behari Ghosh; Extremists want Lala Lajpat Rai (or a face acceptable to Tilak’s camp). Venue, visitors, and a hall that breaks into chaos.
- Tilak is blocked from the platform; shoes and chairs become the memory-image; session collapses.
- Moderates then hold their own meeting, write a constitution that effectively shuts Extremists out of Congress machinery for years.

**Teaching sentence:** Surat is **a split over who owns the national voice**, not a quiz about furniture.

## 2. What Moderates feared, what Extremists refused

Moderates (Gokhale, Pherozeshah Mehta, Banerjea in this quarrel’s older voice):
- Mass boycott and student strikes will invite repression that destroys the slow gains of councils and the press.
- Loyal language still has a British audience after Curzon (Minto is Viceroy from late 1905; Morley is Secretary of State). The next file will teach the Morley-Minto reforms. Today only mark the hope.
- Socially, they still speak for a thin professional class that can be jailed faster than it can raise a province.

Extremists:
- Councils without control of the executive are toys.
- A people that only petitions teaches the Raj that petitions are safe to ignore.
- National education and boycott are how a subject becomes a citizen in his own mind.

Both were describing a real risk. The tragedy of 1907 is that **the movement that had just found mass sound immediately lost a single all-India roof.**

## 3. After Surat — three rooms instead of one

1. **Moderate Congress** — constitutional, smaller, waiting for council reform.
2. **Extremist leaders** — Tilak soon in Mandalay (1908, for sedition after the later violence wave), Pal touring and then quieter, Lajpat Rai in and out of exile and return, Aurobindo toward the Alipore case and then withdrawal.
3. **Revolutionary groups** — Anushilan, Yugantar, later Ghadar elsewhere. Separate lesson. Note only that a split legal movement leaks energy into illegal work when the hall is closed.

The Extremists are not “back in Congress” as a body until the wartime and 1916 Lucknow atmosphere. Do not jump there today.

## 4. All-India Muslim League, Dacca, December 1906

**Why this year, this city:**
- Partition had just given eastern Muslim elites a province worth defending.
- A Simla Deputation (October 1906) to Minto had already asked for separate political recognition of Muslims as a minority that was also, in places, a majority — and for weight in councils and services.
- Nawab Salimullah of Dacca hosts; the League is founded as a loyal, elite, all-India body to protect Muslim political interests and to support, where convenient, the new provincial arrangement.

**Founding character — write it without 1947 hindsight:**
- Not yet a Pakistan resolution. That is 1940.
- Not a mass peasant party. Nawabs, lawyers, professionals.
- Aims in the early wording: loyalty to the British government; protection of Muslim political rights; respectful distance from Congress as the default “national” spokesman.

**Teaching sentence:** 1906 is when **a second all-India address** is invented because the first address (Congress) could not, in Muslim-elite eyes, carry two majorities at once after Bengal was redrawn.

## 5. How to hold 1905–1907 in one paragraph for Mains

Curzon partitioned Bengal in the name of efficiency and produced the first all-India mass agitation of the Congress age. Swadeshi and boycott shifted politics from petition to the market and the school. The same energy broke Congress at Surat because Moderates and Extremists no longer shared a method. In the gap, Muslim elites in Dacca institutionalised a separate claim. The Raj would soon answer all three rooms with council arithmetic. That answer is tomorrow’s weather; today’s file stops at the split.

# BLOCK D — Geography, economy, ethics, current-affairs-explained (40 minutes)

## Geography — the delta is the politics

Draw and name:

1. **Padma–Meghna–Jamuna (Brahmaputra) country** — eastern Bengal’s river maze; jute, rice, silt, floods; Dacca as inland river-city; Chittagong as the sea-gate Curzon offered Assam.
2. **Hooghly–Calcutta** — western industrial-port face; mills, jute presses, ships to Dundee and London; the talking city.
3. **The new inter-provincial line** — not a mountain. A secretariat line across a continuous plain. That is why it felt artificial to Calcutta and useful to Dacca.
4. **Assam tea-and-hill annex** — attached to the east to give the new province size and a “frontier” story. Tea labour and plains politics are different worlds; the map tied them anyway.
5. **Bombay–Ahmedabad mill belt** — far from the cut, but the cloth economy of Swadeshi lives here. Black-soil cotton from yesterday’s files feeds these mills.
6. **Poona–Pune on the Deccan trap** — Tilak’s inland city; festivals as politics because the street, not the port salon, is the theatre.
7. **Punjab canal colonies** — Lajpat Rai’s province: recruitment, irrigation, Arya schools, a different social mix from Bengal bhadralok.

**Physical fact that explains jute politics:** jute wants warm wet delta land. Partition did not move the plant. It moved the *government that taxed and policed the plant*. Calcutta firms still needed eastern fibre. That is why an “eastern province” was an economic as well as communal redesign.

## Economy — boycott as industrial policy by crowd

- Lancashire piece-goods were the boycott’s main target. Every bale refused was a political vote and a price signal.
- Indian mills could not clothe the country in 1905. Shortfall meant either khadi-handloom (limited scale) or leakage back to foreign cloth. Honesty about scale is part of the lesson.
- Jute was the opposite of cotton: raw export from the east, mills on the Hooghly under mostly European capital. Boycott talk sat awkwardly on a crop that lived by export.
- National banks and insurance were attempts to keep Indian savings from the drain-path you studied yesterday. Most early ventures were small; the *argument* mattered more than the balance-sheet in this decade.
- Student time is an economic input: a struck college year is a tax the professional family pays. Extremists asked families to pay it; Moderates said the tax was too high for the yield.

## Ethics — GS-IV from this week’s file

1. **Means and ends.** Extremists argued that meek means produce meek ends. Moderates argued that wild means destroy the end by inviting the soldier. A district officer and a protest movement still live this fight. The usable test: **does the method leave a public that can live together after the demand is won or lost?**
2. **Using community as a map.** Drawing a province so that one community is a majority is an administrative temptation. It treats people as counters. The ethical counter is: administration may notice religion; it may not *weaponise* it to silence a public.
3. **Boycott and the weaker party.** A boycott that raises cloth prices first hurts the household that owns one dhoti. Political morality has to ask who pays for the symbol.
4. **Split versus exit.** Surat shows what happens when a house cannot write rules for disagreement. Organisations that treat dissent as treason manufacture undergrounds.
5. **Loyalty after insult.** Muslim League loyalty in 1906 and Moderate loyalty in 1907 can be prudence, principle, or bargain. Judge by whether the loyal speech still tells the truth about injury.

## Current-affairs-explained (tied to this static file)

When a modern state redraws districts or a state and calls it “administrative convenience,” run the 1905 test: **who was consulted, whose public sphere is broken, and which elite is being gifted a capital?**

When “vocal for local” or a domestic manufacturing drive appears, return to Swadeshi: a slogan without mills, skills, and affordable cloth is a mood, not a policy. A policy without a public is a subsidy.

When campus protests meet rustication, you are watching the 1905 circular habit: the state treats the student as a file, the movement treats the college as a barracks. The adult question is still Gokhale’s: **have you done the homework behind the shout?**

When separate electorates or communal weighting are debated (next file will teach 1909), remember that the *demand* predates the Act: Simla 1906 and Dacca 1906 are already politics of numbers. The statute will freeze a social fear into a machine.

# BLOCK E — CSAT practice with worked solutions (40 minutes)

Attempt first, then read the working.

## Q1. Mixtures

A swadeshi store mixes 30 kg of Indian mill cloth-fibre worth Rs 80/kg with 20 kg of handloom fibre worth Rs 50/kg. At what price per kg must the mix be sold to gain 20%?

**Working.**
Cost \( = 30\times80 + 20\times50 = 2400 + 1000 = 3400 \).
Total kg \( = 50 \).
Cost per kg \( = 3400/50 = 68 \).
Selling price for 20% gain \( = 68 \times 1.20 = 81.6 \).

**Answer: Rs 81.60 per kg.**

## Q2. Pipes

Pipe A fills a municipal tank in 12 hours. Pipe B fills it in 18 hours. Pipe C empties it in 36 hours. A and B are opened together for 4 hours, then C is also opened. How much longer to fill the tank?

**Working.**
A \( = 1/12 \), B \( = 1/18 \), C \( = -1/36 \).
A+B in 1 hour \( = 1/12 + 1/18 = 5/36 \).
In 4 hours: \( 20/36 = 5/9 \).
Left: \( 4/9 \).
Now A+B+C \( = 5/36 - 1/36 = 4/36 = 1/9 \) per hour.
Time \( = (4/9) \div (1/9) = 4 \) hours.

**Answer: 4 hours more.**

## Q3. Coding-decoding

In a certain code, BENGAL is written as DGPICN. How is SURAT written?

**Working.**
B→D (+2), E→G (+2), N→P (+2), G→I (+2), A→C (+2), L→N (+2).
Each letter +2.
S→U, U→W, R→T, A→C, T→V.

**Answer: UWTCV.**

## Q4. Percentages

A mill’s output is 8,000 bales. Boycott raises demand by 25%. Next year a slump cuts the new demand by 20%. What is the final demand relative to the original 8,000?

**Working.**
After +25%: \( 8000 \times 1.25 = 10000 \).
After −20%: \( 10000 \times 0.80 = 8000 \).
Net factor \( = 1.25 \times 0.80 = 1.00 \).
Final equals original. Do not think +25−20 = +5%.

**Answer: 8,000 bales (no net change).**

## Q5. Time and work

Three compositors A, B, C can set a pamphlet in 10, 15 and 30 days. How long if all three work together?

**Working.**
One day: \( 1/10 + 1/15 + 1/30 = 3/30 + 2/30 + 1/30 = 6/30 = 1/5 \).
Days \( = 5 \).

**Answer: 5 days.**

## Q6. Syllogism

Statements: All Extremists are nationalists. Some nationalists are Moderates. No Moderate is a revolutionary.
Conclusions:
I. Some Extremists are Moderates.
II. No Extremist is a revolutionary.
III. Some nationalists are not revolutionaries.

**Working.**
I does not follow: Extremists and Moderates may be disjoint subsets of nationalists.
II does not follow: Extremists need not touch the Moderate set, so the “no Moderate is revolutionary” line does not brand Extremists.
III follows: the Moderates who exist (from “some nationalists are Moderates”) are nationalists who are not revolutionaries.

**Answer: Only III follows.**

## Q7. Ratio

Delegates at a session sit in the ratio Moderates : Extremists : others = 5 : 3 : 2. If there are 120 delegates, how many Extremists?

**Working.**
Parts \( = 10 \). One part \( = 12 \). Extremists \( = 3 \times 12 = 36 \).

**Answer: 36.**

## Q8. Direction sense

A courier leaves Calcutta, goes 8 km east, 6 km north, 8 km west, then 2 km south. How far is he from the start, and in which direction?

**Working.**
East 8 and west 8 cancel.
Net north \( = 6 - 2 = 4 \) km.

**Answer: 4 km north.**

# BLOCK F — Active recall with model cores (25 minutes)

Close the notes. Answer in speech. Then check the cores.

**1. Why was official “efficiency” not enough to explain 1905?**
*Core:* The Presidency was huge, but the line also detached a Muslim-majority east and a new capital from the Calcutta professional public. Motive is read in the political effect, the weak consultation, and Curzon’s wider distrust of that public.

**2. Boycott versus Swadeshi in one contrast.**
*Core:* Boycott refuses foreign goods and sometimes offices and schools. Swadeshi builds Indian goods, stores, and schools. One withholds; the other replaces.

**3. Four Congress words of 1906.**
*Core:* Swaraj, Swadeshi, Boycott, National Education.

**4. Why Surat split.**
*Core:* Fight over President and over method — petition-and-council versus mass boycott and swaraj talk — until the hall broke and Moderates wrote rules that locked Extremists out.

**5. Why Dacca 1906 matters without dragging in 1947.**
*Core:* Eastern Muslim elites institutionalised a loyal all-India body to protect Muslim political weight and the new province, because they did not trust Congress to be their only spokesman after the map changed.

**6. Name Lal-Bal-Pal with provinces.**
*Core:* Lala Lajpat Rai — Punjab; Bal Gangadhar Tilak — Bombay-Poona; Bipin Chandra Pal — Bengal. Aurobindo is the Bengal theorist beside Pal, not the third letter of the slogan.

**7. One economic honesty about Swadeshi.**
*Core:* Indian mills could not meet all cloth demand; jute still lived by export; the poor paid more when prices rose. The political market was real; the industrial replacement was partial.

**8. Map three capitals/gates of the partition file.**
*Core:* Calcutta on the Hooghly; Dacca in the eastern river country; Chittagong as the sea-gate offered to the new province and Assam.
