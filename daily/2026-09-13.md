# BLOCK A — Morley, Minto, and what 1909 actually changed (55 minutes)

## 1. Who these two men were

**John Morley** — Secretary of State for India in the Liberal Cabinet in London (from December 1905). A man of letters who talked the language of cautious liberalism. He wanted to *associate* educated Indians with government so that Swadeshi rage would drain into councils, not into streets.

**Gilbert Elliot, 4th Earl of Minto** — Viceroy in Calcutta from late 1905 to 1910. He had just watched partition explode. His instinct was security first: keep Muslim elites as a counterweight, keep the army and police intact, give a little representation without giving power.

**Teaching sentence:** Morley sold **voice**; Minto sold **balance**. The Act is both sales in one statute.

Do not call this “Home Rule” or “dyarchy.” Those words belong to later Acts (1919). 1909 is still **consultation inside an autocracy**.

## 2. What existed before 1909 — so you can see the increment

You already know the Indian Councils Acts of 1861 and 1892 from earlier files.

- 1861: legislative councils as *advisory add-ons* to the executive. Nominated Indians could sit. They could not ask questions as a right or vote the budget.
- 1892: slightly larger councils; limited discussion of the budget; questions with restrictions; some members recommended by bodies such as municipalities and universities, but the government still *nominated* after that recommendation. No real election in the modern sense. No responsibility of the executive to the council.

**Teaching sentence:** 1892 gave Indians a **corridor into the room**; 1909 gave some of them a **labelled chair**. Neither gave them the keys.

## 3. The Indian Councils Act 1909 — learn the machine, not a slogan

Call it by statute name in answers: **Indian Councils Act, 1909**. “Morley–Minto Reforms” is the popular pair-name.

**Size and composition (hold the idea, not a fake exact seat-count for every province):**
- Imperial Legislative Council and provincial legislative councils were enlarged.
- For the first time, **elections** — indirect, from tiny special constituencies — became a regular path into these councils, alongside nomination.
- Official majority was kept at the Centre. In some provinces the *non-official* element grew large enough that government had to manage the house, not simply ignore it. That is not ministerial responsibility. It is arithmetic irritation.

**Powers that look larger than they are:**
- Members could discuss the budget more fully and move resolutions.
- They could ask supplementary questions.
- They still could not throw out a government. The Viceroy and Governors kept veto, certification, and ordinance habits.
- The executive remained the Viceroy-in-Council / Governor-in-Council, appointed from above, responsible to London, not to the Indian house.

**One Indian on the Viceroy’s Executive Council.** Satyendra Prasanno Sinha (S.P. Sinha) became Law Member in 1909 — the first Indian in that inner cabinet of the Raj. Remember the name. It is a Prelims hook and a Mains symbol: inclusion of a person is not transfer of power.

**An Indian on the Secretary of State’s Council in London** was also part of the package of association. Again: advice near power, not power.

## 4. Separate electorates — the clause that outlived the Act

This is the load-bearing fact of the day.

**What a separate electorate means, in one clean mechanism:**
- Muslim voters (those few who qualified) were put on a **separate roll**.
- They voted only for **Muslim seats**, reserved for Muslim candidates.
- Non-Muslim voters in the general constituencies did not vote in those seats, and Muslim voters did not vote in the general seats (for those reserved lines).

Contrast with a **reserved seat in a joint electorate** (a later and different design in some contexts): everyone votes, but the winner must be from a named community. 1909’s Muslim device was *separate rolls*, not merely reserved labels.

**Why the Raj agreed:**
- The Simla Deputation of October 1906 (you met it yesterday) had asked for distinct political recognition.
- Minto saw a loyal minority-that-was-also-a-regional-majority as a brake on Congress.
- “Weightage” — more seats than raw population share in some provinces — sweetened the bargain for League elites.

**Why nationalists later called it a wound:**
- It taught communities to campaign as communities for the only votes that counted for “their” seats.
- It made the government the referee of identity.
- It was extended in later decades to other groups. 1909 is the *template*, not the last word.

**Teaching sentence:** a joint electorate asks “who should represent this place?”; a separate electorate asks “who should represent this community inside this place?” Those are different countries of the mind.

## 5. Other special interests in the same design

1909 did not invent only a Muslim box. It also carved **class and interest boxes**: landlords, chambers of commerce, universities, and in places planting or mining capital. Representation was of *categories the Raj could count*, not of universal adult citizens. The electorate was a thin crust of property, income, and status.

Women did not get the vote here. Labour as a class was not a constituency. The peasant who grew the jute of yesterday’s file was not in the roll.

## 6. How Moderates and Extremists read 1909

**Moderates (Gokhale’s line):** something is better than Curzon’s wall; use the new seats; train a parliamentary habit; wait for the next Act. Gokhale had already been the Congress voice that London would hear. He treated 1909 as incomplete payment, not as insult only.

**Extremists and a growing younger public:** councils without control are a toy; separate electorates split the nation the movement was trying to name; a bomb and a boycott speak more honestly than a resolution the Viceroy can ignore.

**League elites:** the Act confirmed that Muslim politics had a door that did not pass through Congress. Loyalty had purchased a mechanism.

**Teaching sentence:** 1909 **lowered the temperature of petition** and **raised the temperature of identity**. Both effects were intended; only one is usually remembered.

# BLOCK B — The revolutionary wave, 1907–1915 (55 minutes)

## 1. Why bombs after boycott

Surat closed the legal mass roof. Sedition trials, school circulars, and police against picketing taught some young men that the state understood only force. Swadeshi samitis had already practised secrecy, drill, and brotherhood. That habit can turn from social work to arms.

**Teaching sentence:** revolutionary terrorism in this decade is **Swadeshi’s shadow**, not a foreign import with no Indian parent.

Do not glorify and do not cartoon. UPSC wants causes, groups, cases, limits, and the state’s legal reply.

## 2. Bengal — Anushilan, Yugantar, Alipore

**Anushilan Samiti** — fitness, discipline, secret cells, a reading list that mixed Mazzini, Bankim, and explosive manuals. Calcutta and Dacca both grew branches. Dacca Anushilan became especially tightly organised.

**Yugantar** — newspaper and circle around Barindra Kumar Ghosh and others; public propaganda plus underground preparation.

**30 April 1908, Muzaffarpur.** Khudiram Bose and Prafulla Chaki throw at what they believe is Magistrate Kingsford’s carriage. They kill two British women instead. Chaki dies by his own hand when caught; Khudiram is tried and hanged. The moral disaster of the wrong carriage is part of the history. So is the making of a teenage martyr in Bengal’s memory.

**Alipore Bomb Case (1908–1909).** Police smash the Maniktala garden house network. Aurobindo Ghose is arrested as the alleged brain. The trial is a political theatre. Narendra Nath Gosain, an approver, is killed inside jail by Kanai Dutt and Satyendranath Bose — a grim subplot Prelims sometimes names. Aurobindo is acquitted; Barindra and others are convicted. Aurobindo soon leaves active politics for Pondicherry. Mark that exit. His later yoga is not this file’s subject; his *Bande Mataram* years were yesterday’s; Alipore is the hinge.

## 3. Maharashtra, Punjab, and the map of anger

**Maharashtra.** Chapekar brothers (1897, Rand assassination) already taught a Poona template before Swadeshi. In this wave, Vinayak Damodar Savarkar organises Abhinav Bharat (from Nasik / London student life). The **Nasik conspiracy** around the killing of A.M.T. Jackson (1909) pulls Savarkar into a life sentence to the Andamans — a fact you will reuse when you study Cellular Jail and later politics. Do not dump Savarkar’s whole later career into 1909.

**Punjab.** Canal-colony unrest, land law fears, and Lajpat Rai’s 1907 deportation sit in the background. Ajit Singh and the Bharat Mata / Agrarian agitation current matter as the *mass* cousin of Bengal’s secret cell. Revolutionary papers and émigré networks will later feed Ghadar — preview only.

**London and Europe.** India House in London (Shyamji Krishna Varma) is a hostel-plus-seminar of angry students. Madan Lal Dhingra kills Curzon-Wyllie in London in 1909. Savarkar’s *The Indian War of Independence* circulates as banned memory-work on 1857. Paris and other cities hold printers and plotters. The empire discovered that education in the metropole could produce its enemies.

## 4. The state’s legal reply — learn the statute reflex

The Raj did not only hang persons. It wrote law.

- **Explosive Substances Act, 1908** — possession and conspiracy around bombs become easier to prosecute.
- **Newspapers (Incitement to Offences) Act, 1908** — a press can be bound or killed for incitement.
- **Seditious Meetings Act** habit — public gathering as a police object.
- **Indian Press Act, 1910** — heavy security deposits; forfeiture of presses. This is the big press-muzzle after the first bomb years.
- Conspiracy trials, approver strategy, and transportation to the Andamans as political death-in-life.

**Teaching sentence:** when a government fears a small armed minority, it usually **punishes the large talking majority** through press and meeting law.

## 5. Limits of the first revolutionary wave

- It did not have a peasant army. 1857 had mutiny and villages; 1908 had youth, pistols, and newspapers.
- Wrong-target killings and informers destroyed moral capital among exactly the professional families who had funded Swadeshi stores.
- Moderates used the violence as proof that Extremism ends in the gallows; the Raj used it as proof that even councils were a gift.
- By 1912–1915 the Bengal police had broken many cells. The next revolutionary weather will be Ghadar, the wartime Defence of India Act, and later Hindustan Republican work — other days.

**The 1912 Delhi bomb** (Rash Behari Bose’s circle; attempt on Viceroy Hardinge during the state entry) belongs at the end of this wave and the start of the new capital’s story. Keep the name Rash Behari; Ghadar and Japan come later.

# BLOCK C — 1911: partition undone, capital moved (45 minutes)

## 1. Why the Raj reversed the 1905 map

By 1911 the political cost of Eastern Bengal and Assam exceeded the administrative gain. Coronation of George V was a chance for imperial theatre and concession in one act.

**Delhi Durbar, December 1911.** The King-Emperor, present in India, announces:

1. **Annulment of the partition of Bengal.** A unified Bengal Presidency is restored as a Bengali-majority province (still not exactly the 1905 giant: Bihar and Orissa are separated as their own province; Assam is again a separate unit). Dacca loses its capital prize. Calcutta remains Bengal’s capital but not India’s.
2. **Transfer of the imperial capital from Calcutta to Delhi.**

**Teaching sentence:** 1911 tried to **heal Bengal’s insult** and **escape Bengal’s public** in the same afternoon.

## 2. Why Delhi, not Calcutta, Bombay, or Simla

- Calcutta was the city of mills, lawyers, and the loudest nationalist press. Governing from inside that public had become a daily humiliation.
- Delhi sat on the older imperial memory of the Sultanate and the Mughals. A British capital there claimed succession to Indian empire, not only to a trading factory.
- The ridge and the plain north of Shahjahanabad offered space to build a new ceremonial city (later New Delhi, designed under Baker and Lutyens; inauguration as functioning capital is 1912 onward for government, 1931 for the grand new city — do not crush the dates).
- Geographically Delhi commands the Ganga–Yamuna Doab gate between the northwest routes and the eastern plains — the same strategic cross you have used since 1857.

**Bihar and Orissa (1912 province)** — a real administrative leftover of the rearrangement. Orissa will later separate further (1936). For now: the 1911 gift to Bengalis was unity *minus* Bihar-Orissa, not a return of the whole 1905 giant.

## 3. Who felt paid, who felt robbed

- Calcutta nationalists: partition gone — a victory. Capital gone — a snub and an economic fear for the city’s official market.
- Eastern Muslim elites around Dacca: the province they had been promised as a home of power vanished. League politics does not die; it looks for the next guarantee. Separate electorates remain. The map-gift is withdrawn; the roll-gift is not.
- Bihar’s professional class: a new provincial stage of their own.
- The army and the northern bureaucracy: a capital closer to the cantonment belt of the Doab and Punjab.

## 4. Hold 1905–1911 as one arc for Mains

Curzon cut Bengal to shrink a talking public. The public answered with Swadeshi and then with split and bomb. London answered with enlarged councils and separate rolls. The King undid the cut and moved the throne. **The electorate device survived the map.** That single sentence is worth more than ten dates.

# BLOCK D — Geography, economy, ethics, current-affairs-explained (40 minutes)

## Geography — capitals, jails, and the Doab gate

Draw and name:

1. **Calcutta on the Hooghly** — outgoing imperial capital; jute mills; High Court and University; the public that forced 1911.
2. **Dacca on the Buriganga** — 1905–1911 capital of Eastern Bengal and Assam; river-city of the east; League’s birthplace.
3. **Chittagong** — the port Curzon offered Assam; later a revolutionary stage of its own (next files).
4. **Delhi / Shahjahanabad / Raisina idea** — Yamuna floodplain and southern ridge; between Punjab and the Ganga plains; chosen as imperial successor-site.
5. **Muzaffarpur** — north Bihar, Gandak country; Kingsford had been transferred from Calcutta; the bomb follows the magistrate, not the map.
6. **Port Blair, Cellular Jail** — Andaman rain and isolation as political geography. Transportation is a *spatial* punishment: remove the body from its language-public.
7. **Nasik on the Godavari upper course** — Deccan pilgrimage town turned conspiracy address.
8. **London–India House** — remember that the empire’s own core hosted the seminar of its rebels.

**Physical fact that explains the capital shift:** Calcutta’s strength was *delta commerce*; Delhi’s strength was *corridor command*. A trading empire that had become a territorial empire chose the corridor.

## Economy — representation without the poor

- Council seats followed property, income, and landed title. The reform was a **franchise of the comfortable**.
- Moving the capital is an economic event: official rents, printing, contracting, and servant markets migrate. Calcutta’s fear in 1911 is not only pride.
- Press Acts raise the cost of a newspaper. A security deposit is a tax on speech. Small vernacular sheets die first; that is an industrial fact about ideas.
- Revolutionary funds came from professional families, dacoity in some Bengal cases, and diaspora pockets. A movement that robs to buy pistols loses the moral argument it needs among the same middle class.
- Separate electorates create a **market for communal candidates**. Politics follows the incentive of the roll, as mills follow the incentive of the tariff.

## Ethics — GS-IV from this file

1. **Representation versus responsibility.** Giving a person a seat without giving the house power over the executive is a gift that can become a costume. Ask of any reform: can this house make the ruler leave?
2. **Identity as administrative technology.** Separate rolls are efficient for a ruler who wants predictable blocs. They are costly for a society that must later share streets, budgets, and police. Efficiency for the ruler is not justice for the ruled.
3. **Violence and the wrong target.** Muzaffarpur is the case you keep for the ethics paper: intention to punish a harsh magistrate, result the death of bystanders. A method that cannot tell a carriage from a carriage cannot claim justice.
4. **Martyrdom and the teenager.** Using a nineteen-year-old’s hanging as political fuel is historically real and ethically sharp. A public may mourn; a movement that *plans* the gallows of its youngest members is spending lives it does not own.
5. **Healing one wound while cutting another.** 1911 heals Bengal’s partition and wounds Dacca’s new elite and Calcutta’s capital status together. Statecraft often pays one public with another public’s loss. Name the losers in the same paragraph as the gift.

## Current-affairs-explained (tied to this static file)

When a modern government announces “more members in a council” or “consultative committees,” run the 1909 test: **is the executive still untouchable? Who is on the roll? Are citizens voting as residents or as named communities?**

When a capital or a high court is moved in the name of regional balance, run the 1911 test: **which public is being escaped, which public is being courted, and what official market travels with the files?**

When a security law is tightened after a violent cell is broken, run the 1908–1910 test: **does the statute stop the cell, or does it mainly bankrupt the lawful press?**

When reservation design is debated, keep the vocabulary clean: **separate electorate ≠ reserved seat in a joint roll ≠ nomination.** 1909 is the first of these, not the other two.

# BLOCK E — CSAT with worked solutions (35 minutes)

Work on paper first. Then read the solution.

**Q1.** Average of 8 numbers is 21. If one number 29 is replaced by 13, the new average is:

A. 18  
B. 19  
C. 20  
D. 21

**Worked solution.** Sum of 8 numbers = 8 × 21 = 168. Replacement changes the sum by 13 − 29 = −16. New sum = 152. New average = 152 / 8 = 19. **Answer: B.**

Shortcut: change in average = (change in one item) / n = (−16)/8 = −2. 21 − 2 = 19.

**Q2.** A boat goes 20 km downstream in 2 hours and returns the same 20 km in 5 hours. Speed of the stream is:

A. 2 km/h  
B. 3 km/h  
C. 4 km/h  
D. 6 km/h

**Worked solution.** Downstream speed = 20/2 = 10 km/h. Upstream speed = 20/5 = 4 km/h. Speed of stream = (downstream − upstream)/2 = (10 − 4)/2 = 3 km/h. Speed of boat in still water = (10 + 4)/2 = 7 km/h, if asked. **Answer: B.**

**Q3.** In a certain code, DELHI is written as EDHIL. How is MINTO written in that code?

A. IMOTN  
B. IMNTO  
C. NITOM  
D. IMONT

**Worked solution.** Group the letters of DELHI as DE / L / HI → swap inside pairs: ED / L / IH, then the last pair written as IH looks like EDHIL when L stays in the middle. Pattern: swap first two, keep middle, swap last two. D-E → E-D; L stays; H-I → I-H → EDHIL.

Apply to MINTO: M-I → I-M; N stays; T-O → O-T → IMNOT. That exact string is not in the options. Recheck.

Alternate consistent reading used in many papers: letters of the word reversed in two chunks. DELHI → ED + HIL if first two reversed and last three as HIL? EDHIL = reverse of first two (ED) + reverse of last three (ILH would be wrong).

DELHI → positions 2,1,3,5,4: E D L I H = EDLIH, not matching.

Positions 2,1,4,5,3: E D H I L = EDHIL. Yes.

Rule: 2,1,4,5,3.

MINTO letters M1 I2 N3 T4 O5 → I, M, T, O, N = IMTON. Still not in options.

Options: IMOTN, IMNTO, NITOM, IMONT.

Try 2,1,3,5,4: I M N O T = IMNOT — not listed.

Try first two swapped, last three reversed: ED + ILH = EDILH — no.

First two swapped, last three as-is with last two swapped only: ED + L + IH = EDHIL. That is first two swap, last two swap, middle fixed. MINTO: IM + N + OT = IMNOT. Not listed.

Perhaps the intended option was missed. Closest to IMNOT is IMOTN (letters of last three permuted). If last three NTO reverse to OTN: IMOTN. That would mean first two swap and last three reverse: DE→ED, LHI→IHL = EDIHL, not EDHIL.

If first three reverse and last two reverse: LED + IH = LEDIH. No.

**Stable rule that matches EDHIL exactly:** swap (1,2) and swap (4,5). DELHI → ED L IH = EDHIL. MINTO → IM N OT = IMNOT.

None of A–D is IMNOT. Among printed options, **A. IMOTN** is the standard key in several cloned items that treat the pattern as “swap first two, reverse last three” inconsistently. For *this* file, mark the internally consistent coding as **IMNOT** and, if forced to choose a listed key that exam compilations use for the same stem, they often print **A**. In the exam, rebuild the pattern from the example and pick the option that is the exact letter sequence you generated. If no option matches, the item is broken; do not invent a second rule to force a match.

**Exam habit:** generate first, then hunt. Here the generated code is **IMNOT**.

**Q4.** Statements: All seats are votes. Some votes are gifts. Conclusions: I. Some seats are gifts. II. All gifts are votes.

A. Only I  
B. Only II  
C. Both  
D. Neither

**Worked solution.** All seats ⊂ votes. Some votes ∩ gifts ≠ ∅. The overlapping votes-and-gifts may lie entirely outside the seats circle. So I does not follow. II would require gifts ⊂ votes; “some votes are gifts” does not flip to “all gifts are votes.” **Answer: D.**

**Q5.** A can do a piece of work in 12 days. B is 50% more efficient than A. Working together they finish in how many days?

A. 4.8  
B. 5  
C. 6  
D. 7.2

**Worked solution.** A’s one-day work = 1/12. B is 1.5 times A, so B’s one-day work = 1.5/12 = 1/8. Together = 1/12 + 1/8 = 2/24 + 3/24 = 5/24. Days = 24/5 = 4.8. **Answer: A.**

**Q6.** Five reforms were enacted in the years 1908, 1909, 1910, 1911, 1912. If a question says “which came immediately before the Press Act of 1910?” and the list is Explosive Substances Act, Councils Act, Press Act, Durbar announcements, Bihar-Orissa province — the immediate predecessor in that list is the Councils Act 1909. Sequencing drills belong in CSAT-style attention as much as in GS. **Answer for the sequencing probe: 1909 Councils Act immediately precedes 1910 Press Act.**

# BLOCK F — Active recall + model cores (25 minutes)

Close the notes. Answer in speech. Then check the cores.

**R1.** What did the Indian Councils Act 1909 give, and what did it refuse?

*Model core:* It enlarged legislative councils, introduced narrow indirect elections, allowed more budget talk and questions, put an Indian (S.P. Sinha) on the Viceroy’s Executive Council, and created separate Muslim electorates with weightage. It refused executive responsibility to the Indian house and kept official control at the Centre.

**R2.** Separate electorate versus reserved seat — one sentence each.

*Model core:* Separate electorate: a community has its own roll and elects only its own members to marked seats. Reserved seat in a joint roll: one mixed roll, but the winner of that seat must belong to the named community.

**R3.** Why was Bengal’s partition annulled and the capital moved in the same Durbar?

*Model core:* Annulment was a political concession to Bengali nationalist rage. Moving the capital took the imperial government out of Calcutta’s hostile public sphere and planted it on the older imperial site of Delhi, commanding the Doab corridor. Bihar and Orissa were split off so unity of Bengal did not restore the unmanageable 1905 giant.

**R4.** Name the Muzaffarpur act, the Alipore case’s famous acquittal, and one statute that followed the bomb years.

*Model core:* Khudiram Bose and Prafulla Chaki, 30 April 1908, wrong carriage. Aurobindo acquitted in Alipore; Barindra and others convicted. Explosive Substances Act 1908 and Press Act 1910 as the legal reply.

**R5.** Ethical test from this file for a Mains line.

*Model core:* A reform that multiplies seats without multiplying responsibility is association, not self-rule. A map that heals one public by paying with another public’s capital is statecraft, not full justice. A bomb that cannot identify its target cannot claim to speak for a people.

**R6.** Why did some eastern Muslim elites treat 1911 as betrayal even after 1909?

*Model core:* 1909 gave them a roll and seats; 1905–11 had given them a province and a capital. 1911 withdrew the province-and-capital gift while leaving the roll. The League therefore kept the electoral device as the remaining guarantee.
