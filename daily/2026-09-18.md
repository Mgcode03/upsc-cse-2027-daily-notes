# BLOCK A — Lahore 1929: independence as Congress creed (50 minutes)

## 1. Why dominion language expired

The Nehru Report had asked for **dominion status**: responsible government inside the Empire, on the late-1920s Canadian–Australian pattern. Younger Congress voices — **Jawaharlal Nehru** and **Subhas Chandra Bose** loud among them — said that ceiling was already too low after Simon’s composition and after a year of no British acceptance.

**Teaching sentence:** Dominion status is self-government **under** a Crown link. *Purna swaraj* is independence as the **goal itself**, not a later upgrade that London might grant.

Gandhi had used Calcutta 1928 to buy time, not to kill the demand. When 31 December 1929 arrived without a British yes to the Indian draft, the Lahore session could cash the ultimatum without looking impulsive.

## 2. The session as a political machine

- **Place:** Lahore, on the banks of the Ravi. The city already carried October 1928’s lathi and November’s mourning for Lajpat Rai. Holding the independence session there is geography as argument.
- **President:** **Jawaharlal Nehru**. Age and generation matter. A Motilal-draft year is followed by a Jawaharlal-chair year: father wrote a dominion constitution; son presides over the independence resolution.
- **Date of the resolution’s public theatre:** the midnight move toward 31 December 1929 / 1 January 1930 is the folklore of the flag on the riverbank. For Prelims, lock **Lahore session, December 1929**, **Purna Swaraj resolution**, **Jawaharlal as president**.
- **Working Committee** was authorised to launch a campaign of **civil disobedience** when ready. The resolution is therefore both creed and **blank cheque for a method**.

**Teaching sentence:** Lahore does two jobs in one sitting — it changes the **aim** (independence, not dominion) and it licenses the **next weapon** (civil disobedience), without yet naming salt.

## 3. What “complete independence” meant in 1929 language

Do not modernise it into 1950’s republic in one jump.

- It rejected the idea that India’s constitution would be a gift of the British Parliament after another Simon-style inquiry.
- It did not yet write a finished republican text. The 1930s still contain federation talks, Round Tables, and the 1935 Act. Lahore is a **goal-shift**, not a draft-shift.
- Socialists and youth heard economic transformation; many landlords and liberals heard a dangerous leap; some Muslim leaders heard a Hindu-majority independence project unless minority locks were first signed. Hold all three hearings. A single “nation spoke as one at Lahore” sentence is poor history.

**26 January 1930** was fixed as **Independence Day** for nationwide pledge meetings. The pledge said, in substance: the British government in India had ruined the country economically, politically, culturally; it is a crime against conscience to submit; we therefore withdraw cooperation and prepare to obey the national congress’s call. After 1950 the Republic used the same calendar date for a different legal event (the Constitution coming into force). **Teaching sentence:** 26 January 1930 is a **movement anniversary**; 26 January 1950 is a **constitutional anniversary**. Same date, two machines. Never confuse them in a Prelims option.

## 4. Why Gandhi did not march the next morning

A creed is not a campaign. Gandhi spent January–February 1930 choosing a breach that was:

1. **Illegal but intelligible** to a villager who had never read the Nehru Report.
2. **Non-violent in form** so that police violence would photograph as excess.
3. **Hard for the Raj to concede quickly** without looking weak on revenue, yet **hard to defend morally** because the tax sat on a necessity of life.
4. **Open to women, peasants, town poor, and coastal labour**, not only to lawyers who could argue Section 144.

Salt satisfied those four tests. Council-walkout alone would have looked like 1920 again. A general no-tax without a single emblem would have scattered. A violent raid would have handed the Raj the Chauri Chaura file.

# BLOCK B — Dandi and the Civil Disobedience machine, 1930–31 (55 minutes)

## 1. The salt law in one paragraph

The colonial state claimed a **monopoly** on the manufacture and sale of salt and levied a **duty**. India has a long coast and inland brine; making salt by evaporating seawater is not a factory mystery. The law made a household act a fiscal crime. Gandhi’s eleven-point letter to Viceroy **Lord Irwin** (January 1930) listed wider demands — rupee-sterling ratio, political prisoners, tariff, prohibition of intoxicants, reduction of land revenue, abolition of the salt tax among them. Irwin did not yield. The march became the public answer.

**Teaching sentence:** Salt is a **necessity tax**. Breaking it is civil disobedience aimed at the **legitimacy of a revenue claim**, not at a single packet of crystals.

## 2. The march as logistics and theatre

- **Start:** **Sabarmati Ashram, Ahmedabad, 12 March 1930**.
- **End:** **Dandi**, coastal village in Navsari tract of then Bombay Presidency (today Gujarat), **6 April 1930**.
- **Distance and days:** about **240 miles / 390 km**, **24 days**, walking with a chosen band of ashram companions. Villages on the route became nightly classrooms: why this tax, why non-violence, why women should join later waves.
- **Act at Dandi:** picking up / making salt from the beach, a photographed breach.
- **Spread:** once the emblem was public, breaches multiplied — **Dharasana** salt works (famous lathi charges on non-violent raiders, **Sarojini Naidu** and others in the leadership of later batches), **Wadala** near Bombay, **Sanikatta** on the Karnataka coast, **Midnapore and Contai** in Bengal, **Malabar**, **Tamil** salt pans, **NWFP** under **Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan** (Khudai Khidmatgars — “Red Shirts”) linking frontier non-violence to the same year.

**C. Rajagopalachari** marched in the south (Vedaranyam). **K. Kelappan** on the Malabar coast. Name two wings so the paper cannot trap you into “only Gujarat”.

**Teaching sentence:** Dandi is the **pilot**; the movement is the **copying** along every shore and many inland brine tracts.

## 3. What else Civil Disobedience contained

Do not reduce 1930 to one beach.

- **No-tax and no-revenue** in pockets (Gujarat, parts of UP, Gujarat’s Bardoli memory helping discipline).
- **Boycott of foreign cloth** and liquor shops — older Swadeshi tools reused.
- **Women** in picketing and salt-making at a scale larger than 1920–22. This is a social fact, not a compliment paragraph. Police handling of women satyagrahis became a legitimacy problem for the Raj.
- **Forest law breaches** in some tribal and hill tracts (grazing, fuel) — remember as a parallel illegalism, not as the headline.
- **Chittagong Armoury Raid, April 1930** (**Surya Sen**) — revolutionary violence in the same month as Dandi. Hold it as a **competing method**, not as Gandhi’s left hand.
- **Peshawar / Qissa Khwani Bazaar, 23 April 1930** — firing on Khudai Khidmatgar and city crowds; Garhwal troops’ refusal to fire in a famous episode often cited in military-ethics questions.
- **Sholapur** martial law after mill and street risings. Urban industrial anger rode the same year.

Gandhi was arrested in **May 1930**. The movement did not end; it lost its central negotiator until early 1931.

## 4. Why the Raj both feared and needed a truce

By late 1930:

- Jails were full; that is costly and makes martyrs.
- Cloth and salt revenue leaked.
- **First Round Table Conference** had opened in **London, November 1930**, without Congress. Princes, Liberals, minority delegates, and British parties talked federation and safeguards in an empty chair where the mass movement should have sat.
- Irwin wanted Congress inside the next London talk. Gandhi wanted prisoners out, peaceful picketing allowed, and salt concessions for the poor.

Those mutual needs produce the Pact, not a sudden conversion.

# BLOCK C — First RTC and the Gandhi–Irwin Pact (45 minutes)

## 1. First Round Table Conference (November 1930–January 1931)

London’s logic after Simon: if Indians will not give evidence to a parliamentary commission, invite them as **delegates to a conference** that will precede a statute.

**Who sat:** British party leaders; princely delegates (federation bait); Muslim League and other minority seats; Liberal and justice-party style voices; depressed-class representation beginning to appear as a separate political claim (this becomes the 1932 Communal Award / Poona Pact story — **next files**, do not dump Ambedkar–Gandhi 1932 into today).

**Who did not sit:** Congress, because it was in jail and on the street.

**Teaching sentence:** A constitutional conference **without the mass party** can draft principles; it cannot deliver Indian consent. That is why Irwin bargains in Delhi while London talks.

Outcomes to keep as skeleton: idea of an **All-India Federation** including princes; responsible government in provinces as the working horizon; Centre still guarded on defence and foreign affairs; communal seats still the unsolved arithmetic.

## 2. Gandhi–Irwin Pact, 5 March 1931

Also called the **Delhi Pact**. Learn it as a **ceasefire contract**, not as swaraj.

**Congress / movement side agreed to:**

- Suspend civil disobedience.
- Participate in the **Second** Round Table Conference.

**Government side agreed to:**

- Withdraw ordinances aimed at the movement.
- Release **political prisoners not convicted of violence**.
- Permit peaceful picketing.
- Allow people in coastal villages to **manufacture salt** for village use (the monopoly was not abolished as a fiscal system).
- Restore confiscated property in specified conditions; treat pending prosecutions with a truce logic.

**What it did not do:**

- Did not grant independence or even dominion status as a dated promise.
- Did not empty every jail (Bhagat Singh’s case is the wound inside the truce — execution **23 March 1931**, eighteen days after the Pact; Congress’s failure to secure those lives is a contemporary accusation you must be able to state fairly).
- Did not settle separate electorates or princely federation.

**Teaching sentence:** Gandhi–Irwin is an **exchange of process** (Congress to London; Raj to release and soften salt) for a **pause in illegalism**. It is not the prize Lahore named.

## 3. Karachi Congress, March 1931 — the resolution you will need for GS-II flavour

After the Pact, Congress met at **Karachi** (president **Vallabhbhai Patel**). Two texts matter:

1. Endorsement of the Pact, with pain over the executions.
2. A **resolution on Fundamental Rights and the National Economic Programme** — living wage language, labour, duty of the future state to the poor, civil liberties. This is an ancestor of later rights-and-directive thinking. You do not need article numbers. You need the idea that 1931 Congress tried to tell the country what independence would **do**, not only what it would **end**.

**Teaching sentence:** Karachi 1931 is the movement saying: independence without a social floor is an incomplete creed.

# BLOCK D — Geography, economy, ethics, current-affairs-explained (45 minutes)

## 1. Geography you must be able to draw tonight

1. **Lahore and the Ravi** — December 1929 session; independence as a riverbank claim in the same city as 1928’s lathi.
2. **Sabarmati** — ashram on the Ahmedabad river terrace; start line of 12 March 1930.
3. **The Gujarat coastal march corridor** — Ahmedabad–Nadiad–Anand–Borsad–Ras–Amod–and-southward villages toward the Gulf of Khambhat / open Arabian Sea face at **Dandi**. You need the **west-coast evaporative pan** logic, not every village name.
4. **Gulf of Khambhat** — high tidal range; salt crystallises on mudflats. Physical geography explains why this coast is a salt factory without chimneys.
5. **Dharasana** — salt works as a fortified monopoly site; lathi theatre after Gandhi’s arrest.
6. **Vedaranyam** (Tamil coast, near the Cauvery delta edge) and **Sanikatta** (Kanara) — proof that the movement is a **peninsular coastal chain**.
7. **Peshawar and the Peshawar valley** — Khudai Khidmatgar country; Qissa Khwani as urban bazaar geography of firing.
8. **Chittagong** — hill-port hinterland; armoury raid as eastern revolutionary geography in April 1930.
9. **London** — First RTC; the place that can still convert talk into the next Act.
10. **Karachi** — March 1931 Congress; a port-city of Sind, still inside Bombay Presidency’s old political weather, hosting the rights resolution.

**Physical teaching sentence:** Civil Disobedience 1930 follows the **salt coast and the canal-and-mill interiors**, not a single Gangetic script.

## 2. Economy — why a cheap crystal was expensive politics

- Salt duty is classic **regressive taxation**: the poor consume salt in a narrower band than they consume mill cloth; a tax on salt hits kitchens that never see an income-tax form.
- Monopoly plus duty created **rent** for the state and for licensed works. Breaking the monopoly is an attack on a **revenue design**, which is why Irwin could allow village-use making without killing the whole tariff system in 1931.
- **Cloth boycott** again hits Lancashire and Bombay mill politics differently: Swadeshi helps some Indian mills even as it hurts others tied to imported yarn. Write the split if a Mains question sounds only patriotic.
- Land revenue no-tax in 1930 rides the **depression** that is already arriving in agrarian prices. Civil Disobedience is not only moral theatre; it is timed to a **price crash** that makes cash revenue feel impossible.
- The rupee-ratio demand in Gandhi’s eleven points is a gold-sterling-money story: an overvalued rupee hurts Indian exporters and agriculturists. You need the political meaning (who is the monetary policy for?), not a 1930s banking thesis tonight.

## 3. Ethics — GS-IV from this file

1. **Civil disobedience defined.** Public, announced, non-violent breach of a specific law, accepting penalty, aimed at converting the opponent and the witness. Contrast with stealth crime and with riot. Gandhi’s rule after Chauri Chaura still binds: if your side kills, you own the pause.
2. **Choosing the law you break.** Salt is chosen because it is unjust in distribution and easy to copy. Ethic of **discriminating illegalism** — not “all law is void”.
3. **Means while the other side shoots.** Dharasana volunteers walking into lathis is a wager that **visible suffering** will shift third-party conscience (Indian fence-sitters, British Labour, American press). Ask when that wager exploits bodies, including women’s bodies, as spectacle.
4. **Truce versus comrades on death row.** The Pact’s violence-exclusion clause left Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev, Rajguru outside the bargain. Integrity requires stating Gandhi’s consistent non-violence **and** the charge of abandonment. Do not dissolve one into the other.
5. **Frontier non-violence.** Khudai Khidmatgars challenge the stereotype that Pathan politics equals only the rifle. Ethic: a community is not a method.

## 4. Current-affairs-explained — living structures

- **Regressive consumption taxes.** When a contemporary levy sits on fuel, salt-adjacent essentials, or a poor household’s power slab, ask the 1930 question: is this revenue efficient, or is it legitimacy-expensive?
- **Who sits at the constitutional table.** First RTC without Congress is the ancestor of “stakeholder conferences that omit the street”. Test: can the absentees still veto implementation?
- **Ceasefire contracts.** Gandhi–Irwin is a model of sequenced bargains: prisoners, ordinances, symbolic concessions, return to talks. Modern internal security and protest settlements still use that template. Judge them by what they exclude (violent-offence prisoners, structural demands).
- **National days with two histories.** 26 January is the cleanest example of a date that must be narrated as **two events**. Whenever a current commemoration collapses them, restore the 1930 pledge versus the 1950 legal birth.
- **Coastal commons.** Salt pans, fishing rights, and port-led industrial corridors still collide. Dandi’s mudflat is a reminder that **shoreline chemistry is political**.

# BLOCK E — CSAT with worked solutions (40 minutes)

Q1. A salt duty of 8% is levied on the ex-works price. A licensed work sells at ₹25 per unit before duty. A village-made unit costs ₹18 and pays no duty. By what percentage is the licensed-and-taxed unit costlier than the village unit?

**Worked solution.**  
Taxed price = 25 × 1.08 = 27.  
Difference = 27 − 18 = 9.  
Percentage dearer = (9 / 18) × 100 = **50%**.

Q2. A satyagraha column covers 390 km in 24 days, resting every 8th day completely. On walking days it averages the same distance. What is the average on a walking day? (Assume rest days included in the 24.)

**Worked solution.**  
Rest days in 24 = floor(24/8) = 3 if the 8th, 16th, 24th are rests.  
Walking days = 21.  
Average per walking day = 390 / 21 = **130/7 km ≈ 18.57 km**.

Q3. In a district, 40% of volunteers are women. Of women, 25% picket liquor shops; of men, 10% picket liquor shops. What percentage of all volunteers picket liquor shops?

**Worked solution.**  
Let 100 volunteers: 40 women, 60 men.  
Women picketers = 0.25 × 40 = 10.  
Men picketers = 0.10 × 60 = 6.  
Total = 16%. **16 percent.**

Q4. Statements:  
A. All salt-tax protests are civil disobedience.  
B. Some civil disobedience is violent.  
Conclusions:  
I. Some salt-tax protests are violent.  
II. No salt-tax protest is civil disobedience.

**Worked solution.**  
A says salt-tax protests ⊂ civil disobedience. B says some civil disobedience is violent — not necessarily the salt subset.  
I does not follow. II contradicts A.  
**Neither follows.**

Q5. A jail holds 360 prisoners. One-third are civil-disobedience convicts, of whom 25% are women. How many women CD convicts?

**Worked solution.**  
CD convicts = 360 / 3 = 120.  
Women = 0.25 × 120 = **30**.

Q6. If 12 men can picket 18 shops in 6 days, how many shops can 8 men picket in 9 days at the same rate?

**Worked solution.**  
Man-days per shop = (12 × 6) / 18 = 4.  
8 × 9 = 72 man-days → 72 / 4 = **18 shops**.

Q7. A clock seen at the start of the march shows 6:00. After 240 minutes of walking it is 10 minutes fast. What is the true time elapsed if we only know the clock’s shown interval was 250 minutes? (Use the standard “clock gain” framing: shown 250 min includes 10 min gain.)

**Worked solution.**  
Clock shows 250 minutes while true time is 240 minutes.  
The question’s first sentence already gave true 240. If shown interval = 250 for a 10-minute gain, true = 250 − 10 = **240 minutes**.  
(Do not overcomplicate; UPSC often plants a redundant clause.)

Q8. Syllogism:  
Premises: Some Round Table delegates are princes. All princes want federation safeguards.  
Conclusion: Some Round Table delegates want federation safeguards.

**Worked solution.**  
Some delegates are princes, and all princes want safeguards, so those overlapping delegates want safeguards.  
**Follows.**

# BLOCK F — Active recall + model cores (25 minutes)

**R1.** Why was 26 January 1930 observed, and how is it different from 26 January 1950?  
**Core:** 1930 = Congress Independence Day pledge after Lahore’s purna swaraj. 1950 = Constitution comes into force; Republic Day. Same date, movement versus law.

**R2.** Name start, end, dates, and the emblem of the 1930 march.  
**Core:** Sabarmati 12 March 1930 to Dandi 6 April 1930; salt manufacture / lifting as breach of the monopoly-duty law.

**R3.** What did the Gandhi–Irwin Pact exchange?  
**Core:** CD suspended + Congress to Second RTC versus ordinances eased, non-violent political prisoners released, peaceful picketing, limited village salt.

**R4.** Why salt, not only a council boycott?  
**Core:** Necessity good, copyable by the poor, photogenic non-violent breach, hard for the Raj to defend morally, wider than lawyer-politics.

**R5.** First RTC’s structural defect?  
**Core:** Congress absent; princes and minority and Liberal seats present; consent of the mass movement missing.

**R6.** One eastern and one frontier event of April 1930 besides Dandi?  
**Core:** Chittagong Armoury Raid (Surya Sen); Qissa Khwani / Peshawar firing and Khudai Khidmatgars.

**R7.** Karachi 1931’s extra text besides the Pact?  
**Core:** Fundamental rights and national economic programme resolution — social floor language for the future state.

**R8.** Why is Bhagat Singh’s hanging an ethical stress-test of the Pact?  
**Core:** Violence-exclusion left the Lahore trio outside releases; 23 March 1931 followed 5 March 1931; truce and revolutionary capital sentence collided in public conscience.
