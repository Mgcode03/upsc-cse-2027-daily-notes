# BLOCK A — How the Act arrived and what kind of law it is (40 minutes)

## 1. The conveyor belt, one last time

Lock the sequence without poetry:

**Simon Commission (1927–28) → Nehru Report and Fourteen Points (paper answers) → Round Table Conferences (1930–32) → Communal Award + Poona Pact (1932) → White Paper (1933) → Joint Parliamentary Committee → Government of India Act 1935.**

The Act received royal assent in **August 1935**. Different parts commenced on different dates. The part that actually changed Indian political life first was **provincial autonomy**, brought into operation in **April 1937** after elections. The federal-Centre part was conditional on princely accession and **never commenced**.

**Teaching sentence:** Do not say “1935 created the federation of India.” Say “1935 *wrote* a federation and *ran* the provinces.”

## 2. What kind of document this is

- It is a **British Parliament statute**, not a constituent assembly product. Sovereignty still sits in London. The Crown, the Secretary of State for India, and the Governor-General remain the legal spine.
- It is **long and detailed** because London no longer trusted vague promises. After the mass movements of 1920–22 and 1930–34, the Raj wanted clauses, schedules, special responsibilities, and override switches.
- It **repeals and replaces** large parts of the Government of India Act 1919 machinery, but it does not repeal empire. Dyarchy is **abolished in the provinces** and **introduced at the Centre** (on paper).
- It is the **immediate legal ancestor** of large pieces of the 1950 Constitution: three lists, a federal court idea, an all-India services idea, Governor’s report language, emergency-style overrides. Ancestry is not identity. 1935 has no justiciable fundamental rights chapter of the 1950 kind, no adult franchise as a principle, and no “We the People”.

## 3. Two storeys, two fates

Draw this on scrap paper:

```
        ALL-INDIA FEDERATION (designed, never started)
        British Indian provinces + acceding princely states
        Dyarchy at the Centre: reserved subjects vs transferred subjects
        Condition: a sufficient bloc of princes must accede
                         |
                         X  princes do not accede; World War II arrives
                         |
        PROVINCES (started 1937)
        Autonomy: ministers responsible to elected provincial legislatures
        But Governor keeps special responsibilities and discretionary pockets
```

**Teaching sentence:** The Act is a **two-storey house whose upper floor was never occupied**.

## 4. Why Congress called it a “charter of slavery” and still used it

The public Congress line after 1935: the Act is a cage. Safeguards, Governor’s override, no real Centre, communal electorates, princes as a conservative lock, and the Secretary of State still above the Indian voter. That line is sincere as critique.

The operational Congress line by 1936–37: if the cage has rooms called ministries, enter the rooms, show that Indians can govern, and fight the cage from inside. That is the old Swarajist instinct returning with a mass party behind it.

**Hold both sentences.** Prelims traps say “Congress rejected 1935 and therefore boycotted 1937.” False. Rejection of the *ideal* and use of the *machinery* can live in one party.

# BLOCK B — Provincial autonomy: the part that actually ran (55 minutes)

## 1. End of provincial dyarchy

Under the 1919 Act, a provincial subject list was split: **reserved** subjects (police, finance, land revenue in the classic picture) sat with councillors responsible to the Governor; **transferred** subjects (education, health, local government, agriculture in the classic picture) sat with ministers responsible to the legislature. That split is **dyarchy**.

1935 **abolishes that split inside the province**. In principle, the provincial cabinet is responsible to the provincial legislature for the provincial field. The Governor is still not a rubber stamp. Autonomy means: **the ministry can fall if it loses the house**, not: **the Governor has become a statue**.

**Teaching sentence:** Provincial autonomy ≠ responsible government without a colonial roof. It means the roof is higher and the rooms are larger.

## 2. Who sits in the provincial house

- Legislatures are **enlarged**. Some provinces become **bicameral** (a legislative assembly plus a legislative council); others remain unicameral. Bicameralism here is not a dignity prize. It is a conservative second chamber.
- Franchise expands compared with 1919 but remains **property–tax–education weighted**. This is not universal adult franchise. Women’s vote exists in a limited property and literacy band. Labour and commerce have special seats in the design inherited from the Award world.
- **Communal electorates** continue as modified by Award and Pact: separate rolls for Muslims, Sikhs, Europeans, Anglo-Indians, Indian Christians in the relevant provinces; **reserved seats in a joint roll** for the Depressed Classes after Poona.
- A ministry is normally formed from those who can command the assembly. In 1937 that will produce Congress majorities in several provinces and coalition or League-linked arrangements in others. Park the results for the next file. Today own the **rule**: the house can make and unmake a provincial cabinet, subject to the Governor’s loaded toolkit.

## 3. The Governor’s toolkit — learn this as a list

A 1935 Governor is the Crown’s man in the province. He has:

1. **Normal role** — appoint ministers who command the assembly; act on cabinet advice in the ordinary field.
2. **Special responsibilities** — peace and tranquillity; minorities; services; the legitimate interests of the princely states touching the province; a set of financial and all-India concerns. In these zones he may act **against** ministerial advice.
3. **Discretionary pockets** — specified acts he may take in his discretion, not on advice.
4. **Ordinance power** when the legislature is not in session, and other emergency-style overrides if breakdown is declared.
5. **Power to take over** provincial administration if he is satisfied that the constitutional machinery has failed — the ancestor language of what you already know as Article 356 thinking, without pretending the 1950 article is a photocopy.

**Teaching sentence:** Autonomy is the ministry’s daily desk. The Governor keeps the **alarm switch and the minority-protection switch**.

## 4. What a provincial ministry could actually do

In the provincial list (land, police as a provincial subject in the new division, public health, education, agriculture, local government, forests in the provincial slice, many taxes of local life), a ministry could legislate and spend. That is why 1937–39 Congress ministries will later be examined on prohibition, tenancy, education, and release of political prisoners. Those are provincial-list fights.

What a ministry could **not** safely do: smash the all-India services’ protected position; treat the Governor as a clerk; rewrite the communal roll; touch defence and foreign affairs (those are not provincial); pretend the Secretary of State had vanished.

# BLOCK C — The federation that never started, the lists, the Court (50 minutes)

## 1. All-India federation: design

The Act imagined a federation of:

- the **Governor’s Provinces** of British India, and
- such **princely states** as executed Instruments of Accession.

Accession was **voluntary for states** and **piecemeal**: a prince could accede for some federal subjects and bargain over others. Representation of states in the federal legislature was not on a population-equal democratic formula. It was a princely bloc designed to weigh against a Congress-majority British India.

**Why it never started.** Enough princes did not accede on terms London and the states could both live with. Princes feared democracy leaking inward. Congress feared a federation locked by autocrats. The British government, heading into European war, had no appetite to force the last signatures. **Teaching sentence:** A federation that needs the consent of autocratic courts will stall when those courts smell elections.

## 2. Dyarchy at the Centre (paper only)

Because the federal Centre never commenced, this is a **design question**, still asked in Prelims because it is a clean contrast with 1919.

- **Reserved subjects** at the proposed Centre: defence, external affairs, ecclesiastical affairs, tribal areas — in the Governor-General’s hands, not responsible to the federal legislature.
- **Transferred subjects**: the remaining federal field, to be run by ministers responsible to the federal legislature — in theory.
- The Governor-General also held **special responsibilities** and a bundle of discretionary powers even larger than a provincial Governor’s.

**Contrast lock:**

| Feature | 1919 Act | 1935 Act |
|---|---|---|
| Provinces | Dyarchy | Autonomy (with Governor overrides) |
| Centre | Bureaucratic / limited central legislature, no responsible cabinet in the later sense | Designed dyarchy; **not commenced** |
| Lists | Provincial vs central with fuzz | Three lists, written |
| Princes | Outside the 1919 provincial game | Invited as federal units |
| Court | No Federal Court of this kind | Federal Court created |

## 3. Three lists — the intellectual parent of the Seventh Schedule

The Act writes a **Federal List**, a **Provincial List**, and a **Concurrent List**. Residuary power is **not** given to the units as in a classic compact among equals. Residuary legislative power is assigned toward the **Centre / Governor-General** side in the 1935 design. That is why later Indian federalism is called **Union-leaning** even after 1950: the habit of listing, and the habit of a strong Centre, are already in this statute.

You do not memorise every entry tonight. You memorise the **logic**:

- Federal list: things that must be one country or one empire — defence, external affairs, currency, communications of national character, all-India services in their federal aspect.
- Provincial list: land, police of ordinary life, public health, local government, agriculture, most of the village and district state.
- Concurrent list: fields where both may speak, with a rule for whose law prevails when they clash — the Centre’s law generally winning in a clash, subject to the statute’s own conflict rule.

**Teaching sentence:** 1950 does not invent the idea of three lists. It **democratises the author** of those lists.

## 4. Federal Court of India

Established under the Act and opened in **1937** in Delhi. Three functions to lock:

1. **Original jurisdiction** in disputes between units (federation / provinces / states in the design).
2. **Appellate jurisdiction** from High Courts on specified constitutional and civil questions.
3. **Advisory jurisdiction** — the Governor-General may refer a question of law.

A further appeal could still go to the **Privy Council** in London. The Federal Court is therefore a **roof under another roof**. After 1950 the Supreme Court absorbs and exceeds this body; Privy Council appeals end. For 1935, own the name, the year of opening, and the three functions.

## 5. Other institutional furniture

- **Reserve Bank of India** had already been legislated in 1934 and begins operations in **1935**. Do not merge the RBI Act and the GOI Act into one statute. They are neighbours in time: a central bank for a monetary area that the 1935 Act is trying to federalise.
- **Federal Public Service Commission** and provincial commissions: the services are protected as a “steel frame” the Governor must specially regard. All-India services remain a colonial insurance policy against provincial ministers.
- **Secretary of State for India** continues. Indian ministers do not replace him.
- **Burma** is separated from India under related 1935-period legislation (Government of Burma Act 1935). Aden also leaves the Indian administrative bundle. Map consequence: British India’s eastern and western maritime edges are being trimmed as the Indian statute is written.

# BLOCK D — Geography, economy, ethics, current-affairs-explained (50 minutes)

## 1. Geography: the 1935 provincial map you must be able to name

British India under the Act is not today’s States map. Learn the **Governor’s Provinces** as a working set (the big elected units of 1937):

**Madras, Bombay, Bengal, United Provinces, Punjab, Bihar, Central Provinces and Berar, Assam, North-West Frontier Province, Orissa, Sind.**

Add the special units without mixing them into that electoral set: **Delhi**, **Ajmer-Merwara**, **Coorg**, **British Baluchistan**, and the **Chief Commissioners’** territories. **Orissa** and **Sind** as separate provinces are 1930s creations (Sind carved from Bombay; Orissa as its own province). That carving is examinable.

Physical teaching points, not decoration:

1. **United Provinces** — Ganga-Yamuna doab, the densest peasant-politics laboratory of the next ministries.
2. **Punjab** — canal colonies, Sikh separate electorates, a Unionist rural coalition logic that is not Congress and not yet League-in-office.
3. **Bengal** — delta, communal roll arithmetic at its sharpest, Calcutta capital versus east-Bengal Muslim majority countryside.
4. **Bombay** — mill city plus Deccan hinterland plus, after Sind’s cut, a slimmer presidency.
5. **Madras** — Presidency long coast, non-Brahmin justice politics already in the bloodstream, Cauvery-Krishna agrarian south.
6. **NWFP** — mountain gate, Congress-Khudai Khidmatgar possibility that will shock the League narrative in 1937.
7. **Princely India as holes.** Hyderabad, Mysore, Travancore, Kashmir, the Rajputana and Central India agencies — large coloured blanks on the voter map of 1937. Federation failed because these blanks refused to become ordinary units.
8. **Burma out, Aden out.** The Bay of Bengal and the Red Sea are no longer “India Office miscellaneous” in the same way. Indian Ocean geography is being administratively sorted just as the Indian constitution is being federalised on paper.

**Teaching sentence:** 1935 politics is a **map of eleven large provinces plus a constellation of states that would not join**.

## 2. Economy — who pays for autonomy

- Provincial autonomy without a fair **tax split** is a stage set. Land revenue, some excises, and local taxes sit closer to provinces; customs, currency, and the big imperial receipts sit closer to the Centre. Niemeyer Award (1936) is the finance-settlement name often attached to how resources were shared as 1937 approached. You need the idea, not a ledger: **provinces got more room and still needed central transfers**.
- A ministry that promises tenancy reform or prohibition is making a **budget speech**. Prohibition cuts excise; tenancy reform can cut landlord political finance and unsettle credit. GS-III habit: every social promise has a revenue sign.
- The early-1930s slump is not over in village memory. Price of raw farm goods, rural debt, and labour wages shape who walks into the 1937 polling booth among the still-narrow franchise.
- All-India services and military charges remain a **fixed claim** on Indian revenues. A provincial minister cannot redirect the army budget to primary schools.
- RBI 1935 means monetary policy is being centralised just as political speech is being provincialised. That combination — central money, provincial politics — is the modern Indian habit in embryo.

## 3. Ethics — GS-IV from this statute

1. **Safeguard versus trust.** London says safeguards protect minorities and stability. Nationalists say safeguards mean “we do not trust you with power.” A fair ethics paragraph names both: vulnerable groups do need locks; locks written by an empire also protect the empire.
2. **Using an unjust instrument justly.** Entering 1937 office under a statute you have condemned is either hypocrisy or responsible gradualism. The test is what you do with the desk: release prisoners, rewrite tenancy, or only occupy cars.
3. **Princes and subjects.** A federation that gives courts a veto over subjects’ citizenship is a federation of rulers, not of people. Representation without the represented is the same problem you met in 1932 with trustee claims over the Depressed Classes.
4. **Civil-service independence versus civil-service impunity.** Protecting officers from ministerial spite can be a rule-of-law value. Protecting officers from ministerial policy can be a colonial value. Write the line between them.
5. **Law without consent of the governed as authors.** 1935 is legally sophisticated and democratically illegitimate as a founding text. Technical quality does not wash authorship.

## 4. Current-affairs-explained — living structures

- **Article 356 language** did not fall from the sky in 1950. When a contemporary state–Centre clash is framed as “breakdown of constitutional machinery,” you are hearing 1935 Governor-takeover grammar in a republican mouth. The ethical question remains: is the switch protecting citizens or punishing a rival party?
- **Seventh Schedule fights** (farm laws, police, education, simultaneous elections logistics) are list-fights. 1935 taught India to argue by list.
- **Union territories and special units** have a cousin in Chief Commissioners’ provinces: places run more directly from the Centre. When a Union Territory’s assembly is frozen or rewritten, ask 1935’s question: is this unit a member or a holding?
- **All-India services** still sit across State cabinets. The steel-frame debate is not antiquarian.
- **Fiscal federalism** — Finance Commissions, cesses, GST Council bargaining — is the grandchild of “autonomy without the customs till.”
- **Instrument of Accession** will return as the 1947 princely file. Today you only need to know that 1935 tried accession first as a federal construction device and failed.

# BLOCK E — CSAT with worked solutions (40 minutes)

Q1. A province has 250 assembly seats. Community M has a separate roll electing 30 seats. Community D has 36 reserved seats in the joint roll. Special interests take 14 seats. How many general unreserved seats remain in the joint-roll field after removing M’s separate block and the special-interest block from the house total, then removing D’s reserved slice from what is left?

Worked solution.  
House = 250.  
Remove separate M seats and special seats from the house first if the question wants the joint-roll field: 250 − 30 − 14 = 206 joint-roll seats.  
Reserved D = 36, so unreserved general = 206 − 36 = **170**.

Q2. Franchise: 12% of adults in a province of 40 lakh adults can vote. Women are 48% of adults but only one-quarter of the voters. How many women voters are there?

Worked solution.  
Adults = 40,00,000. Voters = 12% of 40,00,000 = 4,80,000.  
Women voters = (1/4) × 4,80,000 = **1,20,000**.  
(Women adults = 0.48 × 40,00,000 = 19,20,000, of whom only 1,20,000 vote — a turnout-eligibility gap you can quote in a Mains line.)

Q3. A provincial budget: land revenue Rs 18 crore, excise Rs 6 crore, other provincial receipts Rs 4 crore. Central transfer Rs 5 crore. Prohibition will delete the excise line. By what percent do total receipts fall if transfers stay fixed?

Worked solution.  
Old total = 18 + 6 + 4 + 5 = 33.  
New total = 18 + 0 + 4 + 5 = 27.  
Fall = 6 on 33 = 6/33 = 2/11 ≈ **18.18%**.

Q4. A ministry needs majority in a 215-member house (ignore Speaker). Allies hold 40. Own party holds 78. How many extra members must be borrowed from a pool of 60 independents?

Worked solution.  
Majority mark = 108.  
Present = 78 + 40 = 118, already past 108.  
**Zero extra.** Trap: candidates add 78+40 and compare to 215/2 without checking they already crossed.

Q5. If they instead had only 62 of their own plus 40 allies?

Worked solution.  
62 + 40 = 102; need 108; borrow **6** from the 60.

Q6. Time-work. Three officers A, B, C file a provincial bill file in 12, 18 and 36 days. A and B start; C joins from day 4. When does the file close? (Assume equal-day output.)

Worked solution.  
Rates: A = 1/12, B = 1/18, C = 1/36.  
Three days of A+B: 3(1/12 + 1/18) = 3(3/36 + 2/36) = 3(5/36) = 15/36 = 5/12.  
Left = 7/12.  
A+B+C per day = 1/12 + 1/18 + 1/36 = 3/36 + 2/36 + 1/36 = 6/36 = 1/6.  
Days more = (7/12) ÷ (1/6) = (7/12)×6 = **3.5 days**.  
Total time = 3 + 3.5 = **6.5 days**.

Q7. Ratio. Federal list items : provincial list items in a toy schedule = 3 : 5. Concurrent is 40% of provincial count. If provincial count is 60, what is the total of all three?

Worked solution.  
Provincial = 60, so federal = (3/5)×60 = 36. Concurrent = 0.4 × 60 = 24. Total = 60 + 36 + 24 = **120**.

Q8. Syllogism.  
Statements: All 1935 Governors have special responsibilities. Some officers with special responsibilities are elected ministers.  
Conclusions: (I) Some 1935 Governors are elected ministers. (II) All elected ministers have special responsibilities.

Worked solution.  
“All G are S. Some S are M.” The overlap of S and M need not touch G. (I) does not follow. (II) claims all M are S, which is not given. **Neither.**

Q9. Statements: No princely state is an acceding unit in 1935 practice. Some designed federal units are princely states.  
Conclusions: (I) Some designed federal units are not acceding units in practice. (II) No designed federal unit accedes.

Worked solution.  
Designed units include princes; those princes, in the “never started” reading of this toy item, do not accede. (I) follows. (II) over-claims (provinces were designed units too). **Only I.**

Q10. Coding. In a file code, AUTONOMY is written as C WVQPQOA. If each letter moves +2 and the string is then reversed, code FEDERAL.

Worked solution.  
Check AUTONOMY: +2 → C WVQPQOA, which is the reverse of C WVQPQOA…  
A→C, U→W, T→V, O→Q, N→P, O→Q, M→O, Y→A. That string is CWVQPQOA. Yes: +2 then the result is already written as that left-to-right string, **not reversed**.  
Wait — CWVQPQOA is exactly +2 in order. So the rule is **+2 only**.  
FEDERAL +2 = HGFGTCN.  
**HGFGTCN.**

(If an option set assumes reverse-after-shift: +2 = HGFGTCN, reverse = **NCTGFGH**. Read the stem. Here the worked check on AUTONOMY shows **no reverse**. Answer **HGFGTCN**.)

# BLOCK F — Active recall + model cores (25 minutes)

Close the notes. Answer in speech. Then match cores.

**R1.** Did the 1935 all-India federation commence?  
Core: No. Provincial autonomy commenced (1937). Federation needed princely accession and never started.

**R2.** Where did dyarchy go?  
Core: Ended in the provinces; designed for the Centre; Centre design unimplemented.

**R3.** Name the three legislative lists.  
Core: Federal, Provincial, Concurrent. Residuary leaned to the Centre/Governor-General side.

**R4.** What is a 1935 Governor’s special responsibility, in one line?  
Core: Zones (peace, minorities, services, certain all-India and princely concerns) where he may override ministers.

**R5.** Federal Court: year of opening and three functions.  
Core: 1937; original (unit disputes), appellate (from High Courts), advisory (Governor-General reference). Privy Council still above it.

**R6.** Does Poona Pact repeal all separate electorates in 1935 houses?  
Core: No. It removed a separate Depressed Class roll in favour of reserved joint-roll seats. Muslim, Sikh, European and other separate rolls continued.

**R7.** Two provinces newly standing as separate Governor’s Provinces in this era?  
Core: Sind (from Bombay) and Orissa.

**R8.** Why can Congress condemn the Act and still fight 1937?  
Core: Condemn the authorship and safeguards; use the provincial desk as a demonstration of Indian capacity and a platform of reform.

**R9.** One economic constraint on a “prohibition ministry”.  
Core: Excise revenue collapses; transfers and land revenue must carry the budget.

**R10.** Ethical one-liner on safeguards.  
Core: A lock can protect a minority or protect an empire; you must say which lock you are looking at.
