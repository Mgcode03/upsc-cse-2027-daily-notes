# BLOCK A — From statute to polling booth (40 minutes)

## 1. Why there is an election at all

The 1935 Act is useless as politics until someone sits in the assemblies. Provincial autonomy commences after elections. Polling for the provincial legislatures is held in **early 1937** (the usual compact date you should write is **February 1937**, with counting and ministry-formation stretching into **July 1937** as office-acceptance is settled).

Franchise is still **narrow**. Rough order of magnitude to lock: about **one-sixth** of the adult population is on the rolls — property, tax, and education tests, plus special labour and commerce seats, plus communal rolls as modified by the Communal Award and the Poona Pact. This is larger than 1920–23 council electorates and still nowhere near universal adult franchise. When a textbook says “the people voted,” translate: **the propertied, the taxed, the literate, and the reserved-category slices voted**.

**Teaching sentence:** 1937 is a mass-party contest on a **restricted roll**.

## 2. Who contests

- **Indian National Congress** — now a mass organisation after Non-Cooperation and Civil Disobedience, with a Working Committee, provincial committees, and a public programme that still says the 1935 Act is inadequate. Contesting does not mean blessing the Act. It means using the rooms.
- **Muslim League** — reorganised under Jinnah in the mid-1930s, still uneven on the ground. Stronger in some minority-Muslim provinces as a spokesman; weaker as a peasant machine in Muslim-majority countryside where local parties already exist.
- **Unionist Party** in Punjab — rural, cross-communal landlord-peasant coalition logic, not a Congress clone and not a League clone.
- **Krishak Praja** type formations in Bengal — tenancy and peasant rhetoric in a communal arithmetic province.
- **Justice Party / non-Brahmin** streams in Madras — older presidency social politics that Congress must absorb or defeat.
- **Liberals, landlords, independents, European and commerce seats** — the conservative furniture of the 1935 house.

**Teaching sentence:** 1937 is not a two-horse race everywhere. Punjab and Bengal have their own rural parties; Congress is the only organisation that is national.

## 3. The result, as a map, not as a slogan

Own this distribution. Exact seat totals vary slightly by source tables; Prelims wants **pattern**, Mains wants **meaning**.

Congress emerges as the largest party in British India as a whole and can form ministries on its own in a clear bloc of provinces:

**Madras, United Provinces, Bihar, Central Provinces and Berar, Orissa, Bombay** — the classic **six** where Congress majority is straightforward.

Then two more that you must not drop:

- **North-West Frontier Province** — Congress (with Khudai Khidmatgar / Red Shirt alignment under Abdul Ghaffar Khan’s movement) can form a ministry. This wrecks any lazy sentence that “Muslim-majority provinces rejected Congress.”
- **Assam** — coalition / Congress-led arrangement rather than a clean walkover; still counted in the Congress-office story.

That is why notes say **eight provinces** under Congress or Congress-led ministries by mid-1937.

Provinces Congress does **not** run:

- **Punjab** — Unionists under Sikandar Hayat Khan.
- **Bengal** — Krishak Praja–League coalition under Fazlul Huq (Huq is the peasant face; League is the partner).
- **Sind** — shifting coalitions; League influence without a simple Congress ministry.

**Teaching sentence:** Write “Congress ministries in eight provinces, not in Punjab, Bengal, or Sind,” and you have the 1937 map.

## 4. What the numbers did to minds

For Congress, 1937 looks like proof that the nation has a party. For the League, 1937 looks like proof that a Muslim voter in a Hindu-majority province cannot expect a share of the cabinet as of right. Both readings are politically real. Neither is a full census of India. The franchise is restricted; princely India did not vote; NWFP voted Congress; Bengal’s countryside voted a peasant-Muslim combination, not a Calcutta Congress committee.

**Prelims trap:** “Muslim League won all Muslim seats.” False. League performance is patchy. It does not sweep the Muslim-majority countryside.

**Prelims trap:** “Congress refused to contest because it had called 1935 a charter of slavery.” False. It contested, then argued about office.

# BLOCK B — Office acceptance and the ministries at work (55 minutes)

## 1. The inner Congress fight

After results, two sentences collide inside Congress:

- **Do not take office.** The Act is a cage. Governors keep special responsibilities. Accepting office educates the public to love a half-freedom and dirties the party with police and land-revenue files.
- **Take office.** The voter has given a desk. Show that Indians can run education, health, jails, and tenancy. Use the cage’s rooms. If the Governor blocks you, the public will see the block.

Gandhi’s public language is cautious; Nehru’s is suspicious of office; the parliamentary wing and many provincial bosses want the desk. The Working Committee line that wins by **March–July 1937** is conditional acceptance: form ministries where Congress has a majority, but treat office as a **weapon inside the statute**, not as loyalty to the statute.

A practical trigger: assurances are sought that Governors will not use special powers as a daily veto. The assurances are political, not a repeal of the Act. Ministries then take oath.

**Teaching sentence:** Office acceptance is **conditional collaboration**, not conversion to the 1935 gospel.

## 2. Who sits in which chair — names you should not fumble

You need a short name-map, not a directory:

- **U.P.** — Govind Ballabh Pant as Premier (the period word is often **Premier**, not Chief Minister).
- **Bihar** — Sri Krishna Sinha.
- **Bombay** — B.G. Kher.
- **Madras** — C. Rajagopalachari (Rajaji).
- **C.P. and Berar** — Ravi Shankar Shukla.
- **Orissa** — Biswanath Das.
- **NWFP** — Dr Khan Sahib (brother of Abdul Ghaffar Khan).
- **Assam** — Gopinath Bordoloi in the Congress-led phase of the story.

Nehru himself does not take a provincial premiership. He remains the national voice, often the critic of compromise, while Pant and Rajaji become the test of whether a Congressman can also be an administrator.

## 3. What they actually try to do

Lock the programme as five files on a desk:

**1. Political prisoners and civil liberty.** Release of satyagrahis and conspiracy-case prisoners where the provincial list allows. Police files and Governor anxiety follow immediately. A nationalist ministry that inherits the colonial police discovers that “freedom inside the statute” still needs handcuffs for communal riots and for left militants.

**2. Tenancy and rural debt.** Rent reduction, protection against eviction, debt-relief talk. This is the peasant payment for 1920–34 mass work. Landlords in the second chambers and in the services resist. A ministry that moves too fast loses revenue and credit; a ministry that moves too slow loses Kisan Sabha patience. U.P. and Bihar are the laboratories.

**3. Prohibition and excise.** Rajaji’s Madras is the emblem: toddy and arrack as moral targets. Excise is also a **budget pillar**. Prohibition is ethics plus a hole in the treasury. GS-III sentence: social reform that kills a vice-tax must name a replacement tax.

**4. Education, local bodies, temple and public-access questions.** Expansion talk, basic education experiments in the Gandhi–Zakir Husain line, and provincial social legislation. Madras also becomes the site of temple-entry and Hindi-south language friction — a reminder that a national party in a presidency is not a culturally neutral clerk.

**5. Labour and municipal life.** Bombay’s mill world expects something from a Congress ministry. What it gets is limited: labour is concurrent-ish in practice and capital can flee. Ministries mediate more than they socialise industry.

**Teaching sentence:** 1937–39 is not Swaraj. It is **jail-keys, rent files, toddy shops, and school grants under a Governor’s roof**.

## 4. What they cannot do

- Defence, external affairs, and the all-India services’ protected core are not theirs.
- They cannot abolish communal electorates.
- They cannot treat the Governor as a ceremonial mascot when he cites peace, minorities, or services.
- They cannot print money or rewrite customs.
- They cannot walk into a princely state and hold an election.
- They cannot satisfy the Congress Socialist / Kisan left and the landlord-right of the same party on the same Tuesday.

The experiment therefore trains a generation of Indian ministers in **budget arithmetic and police files** — useful for 1946–50 — and also trains them in **frustration**, which feeds the 1939 walkout.

# BLOCK C — League, war, resignation (50 minutes)

## 1. Coalition refusal in U.P. and the “Hindu Raj” charge

After 1937, the sharpest political wound is not a riot statistic. It is **cabinet arithmetic in the United Provinces**.

Congress has a majority on its own in U.P. The League wants a coalition as recognition that Muslims are a separate political nation entitled to a share of the executive, not merely to reserved legislative seats. Congress, thinking like a parliamentary majority party, says: join Congress, accept Congress discipline, then sit in the cabinet — there will be no coalition with a separate communal party as an equal.

To Congress this is the logic of a single nation and a single cabinet. To Jinnah this is proof that majority rule under a joint nationalism means **permanent minority status** in the executive of the Hindu-majority heartland.

**Teaching sentence:** 1937 turns a **seat-share dispute** into a **sovereignty dispute**.

You must hold fairness in both hands for Mains:

- Congress is not obliged by Westminster arithmetic to take a minority party into cabinet when it already has a majority.
- A minority that has just been told “come in as Congressmen or stay out” can honestly hear that as assimilation, not partnership — especially after two decades of separate electorates.

Mass-contact campaigns, cow-protection atmosphere in places, school-song fights, and volunteer-corps optics then give the League daily illustrations for a pamphlet war. “Congress Raj is Hindu Raj” is a **political construction** that feeds on real slights and invented ones. Do not wash it as pure falsehood; do not swallow it as a court verdict.

## 2. 1938–39: the party in office grows tensions

Inside Congress: Bose is elected President at Haripura (1938) and again in a contest that leads toward Tripuri (1939) and his exit toward Forward Bloc. That story is the next file’s centre; today only park the fact that **office does not end faction**. Socialists want faster tenancy and labour moves; right-Gandhians want order and constructive work; provincial bosses want the ministry to survive.

Outside Congress: League session politics hardens. The federal Centre still has not started. Princes still have not acceded. The statute’s upper floor remains empty while the lower floor is noisy.

## 3. 3 September 1939 and Linlithgow

When Britain declares war on Germany, the **Viceroy, Lord Linlithgow, declares India at war** without consulting the provincial ministries or any Indian national body that could claim to speak for the electorate of 1937.

That single act is the constitutional insult the ministries have been waiting for, or dreading. It proves the teaching sentence of yesterday: autonomy is a desk, not sovereignty. Defence and war are reserved imperial facts.

Congress asks: what is India’s war aim? Is this a war for democracy that will include India’s democracy? Linlithgow’s response, culminating in a limited October 1939 statement of vague postwar talk, does not give a constituent-assembly promise of the kind Congress can sell to its own left or to Gandhi’s moral ledger.

## 4. Resignation, October 1939

Working Committee direction: **Congress ministries resign**. Through October 1939 the eight-province experiment ends. Governors take over provincial administration under the Act’s breakdown / Governor-rule machinery. League ministries or League-friendly arrangements in non-Congress provinces can claim continuity. Jinnah calls for a **Day of Deliverance** (22 December 1939) to mark relief from Congress office — a sentence that tells you how badly 1937–39 had poisoned all-India conversation.

**Teaching sentence:** Ministries resign over **who has the right to declare a people’s war**, not over a failed school grant.

Consequences to lock tonight:

1. Congress leaves the administrative machine just as war demand, inflation, and later famine will need administrators. The vacuum is real.
2. League can tell its audience: we were shut out of 1937 cabinets and we outlasted those cabinets.
3. Gandhi will next look for a moral stance short of full revolt (Individual Satyagraha, 1940) while Bose looks for a harder road. Tomorrow’s files own those forks.
4. The Cripps idea of 1942 and the Quit India explosion only make sense if you remember that Indians had already tasted office and then been told that war is still an imperial telegram.

# BLOCK D — Geography, economy, ethics, current-affairs-explained (50 minutes)

## 1. Geography: why the same statute produced different ministries

Draw eight boxes and three refusals.

**United Provinces** — doab density, taluqdars and occupancy tenants, a Congress peasant vocabulary that must still collect land revenue. This is why U.P. cabinet composition becomes the national quarrel: U.P. is the demographic prize of the Hindi heartland.

**Bihar** — permanently settled scars plus Kisan Sabha pressure. Tenancy files here are not theory.

**Madras Presidency** — long coast, Cauvery-Krishna agrarian south, a Justice Party past, a Hindi-imposition fight waiting in the school file, toddy-palm ecology that makes prohibition a landscape policy.

**Bombay** — mill city on the west-facing coast plus Deccan dry farms. Labour geography and cotton-price geography sit in one ministry.

**C.P. and Berar** — cotton-wheat interior, forest and tribal edges, a reminder that “Hindi-heartland Congress” is not only U.P.

**Orissa** — a young province (separated in the 1930s), coastal-delta plus highland tribal tracts. A ministry here is state-building, not only ideology.

**Assam** — Brahmaputra valley, tea, migration politics already in the bloodstream, a coalition geography.

**NWFP** — Khyber gate, irrigated Peshawar vale versus tribal hills, Khudai Khidmatgar social base. A Congress-coloured ministry on the Afghan edge is a map fact the League pamphlet cannot delete.

**Punjab not taken** — canal colonies, Sikh separate roll, Unionist rural coalition. Wheat-and-water politics beats Congress language in 1937.

**Bengal not taken** — tidal delta, east-Bengal Muslim peasant majority, Calcutta professional Congress. Huq’s Krishak Praja is the agrarian key; League is the partner.

**Sind not taken** — Indus lower basin, Karachi port, a thin new province after the cut from Bombay.

**Princely holes** — Hyderabad, Kashmir, Mysore, Travancore still have no 1937 voter. Office in British provinces does not equal office in India.

**Teaching sentence:** 1937 is an election of **eleven British provinces**, not of the subcontinent.

## 2. Economy — ministries as budgets

- Land revenue remains the provincial backbone. Tenancy relief that cuts landlord power can also cut collections and unsettle rural credit. A serious answer names **who pays** when rent falls.
- Prohibition cuts **excise**. Madras must then squeeze other heads or shrink expenditure. Moral politics is fiscal politics.
- The late-1930s farm-price recovery from the slump is incomplete. Ministries inherit indebted tenants, not a boom.
- Bombay labour wants wages; Bombay capital wants order and tariff sympathy the province cannot legislate.
- War, from September 1939, will soon mean **procurement, inflation, and shipping scarcity**. Resigning ministries hand those war-economy files back to Governors just as the files get lethal. The 1943 Bengal famine is not “caused by 1939 resignation,” but resignation is part of the administrative weather in which war economy operates.
- Niemeyer-style transfers still matter. Provincial autonomy without an elastic tax base is a short leash.

## 3. Ethics — GS-IV from office

1. **Integrity in a tainted instrument.** Taking oath under a statute you have condemned: hypocrisy, or responsibility to the voter? The test is conduct — release of prisoners, communal fairness, refusal to use police as a party militia.
2. **Majority rule versus minority dignity.** Cabinet exclusion can be constitutionally normal and ethically brutal at the same time. Write both clauses.
3. **Police power.** A freedom movement that inherits the lathi must decide whether colonial public-order doctrine is now “our law.” Ends and means return from the Swadeshi file.
4. **Resignation as conscience.** Walking out over an undeclared-in-India war is a clean moral sentence. It is also an abdication of famine-and-inflation desks. Conscience can create a vacuum.
5. **Deliverance language.** Celebrating an opponent’s exit as “deliverance” is permitted politics and poisonous civic ethics. Name the poison without denying the grievance that manufactured it.

## 4. Current-affairs-explained — living structures

- **State governments resigning on a Union question** is still Indian politics. 1939 is the ancestor of “we will not implement a Centre decision we did not author.” The legal wrap is different after 1950; the reflex is older.
- **Coalition arithmetic in U.P.** remains the prize of North Indian politics. When you read a contemporary seat-share fight in Lucknow, you are in the same doab that taught India how cabinet exclusion becomes identity.
- **Governor’s rule / President’s rule** after a ministry walks out is 1935 machinery in republican clothes. Ask who benefits from the vacuum.
- **Prohibition experiments** in Indian states still collide with excise dependence. Rajaji’s ledger is not antique.
- **War powers and consultation.** A democracy that can be committed to a conflict without its elected provincial executive is the 1939 complaint. Modern analogues are parliamentary debate over external deployments and ordinances.
- **Administrative continuity in crisis.** The ethical case for staying in office during a war-famine risk is the case officials still make when parties want a walkout.

# BLOCK E — CSAT practice with worked solutions (45 minutes)

Attempt first, then read the working. Prelims CSAT is qualifying; speed with zero ego.

## Q1. Percentages

In a 1937-style restricted electorate of 12,00,000 voters, 40% vote. Congress takes 55% of votes polled in a province. How many votes does Congress get?

**Worked solution.**  
Votes polled = 40% of 12,00,000 = 4,80,000.  
Congress = 55% of 4,80,000 = 55 × 4,800 = 2,64,000.

## Q2. Ratios

Seats in a house are shared by Congress, League, and Others in the ratio 9 : 2 : 5. If Others have 40 seats, how many does Congress have?

**Worked solution.**  
Others = 5 parts = 40 ⇒ 1 part = 8.  
Congress = 9 × 8 = 72.

## Q3. Two-step percentage

A ministry cuts excise receipts by 20% after prohibition. The next year remaining excise grows 10% on the new base. What is the net change from the original excise?

**Worked solution.**  
Take original = 100.  
After cut = 80.  
After 10% growth = 88.  
Net = 12% fall.  
Do not add −20 + 10 = −10. Bases changed.

## Q4. Time and work

Three clerks A, B, C file tenancy cases. A files a stack in 12 days, B in 18, C in 36. How many days if all three work together?

**Worked solution.**  
One-day work = 1/12 + 1/18 + 1/36.  
LCM of 12, 18, 36 = 36.  
3/36 + 2/36 + 1/36 = 6/36 = 1/6.  
Together = 6 days.

## Q5. Syllogism

Statements:  
All 1937 Congress ministries are provincial governments.  
Some provincial governments are coalition governments.  
Conclusions:  
I. Some 1937 Congress ministries are coalition governments.  
II. All coalition governments are provincial governments.

**Worked solution.**  
I does not follow: the “some coalitions” may lie entirely outside the Congress-ministry circle.  
II does not follow: coalitions can exist outside this universe of discourse as stated.  
Answer: **neither**.

## Q6. Direction sense

A campaigner walks 6 km north, 8 km east, 6 km south. How far is he from the start, and in which direction?

**Worked solution.**  
North 6 and south 6 cancel. Net = 8 km east.  
Distance = 8 km east.

## Q7. Simple vs compound comparison

A provincial loan of ₹10,000 at 10% per annum. Difference between CI and SI after 2 years (annual compounding)?

**Worked solution.**  
SI for 2 years = 2,000.  
CI = 10,000 × 1.1 × 1.1 − 10,000 = 2,100.  
Difference = ₹100  
(Which is also SI on the first year’s interest: 10% of 1,000.)

## Q8. Data sufficiency

Is Congress the majority party in province X?  
(1) Congress has 60 seats.  
(2) The house has 110 seats.

**Worked solution.**  
Majority means more than half of 110 = at least 56.  
(1) alone: 60 is majority only if we know house size.  
(2) alone: no seat figure.  
Together: 60 > 55, yes.  
Answer: **both needed**.

# BLOCK F — Active recall + model cores (25 minutes)

Close the notes. Answer in speech. Then match the core.

**1. Did Congress reject the 1935 Act and therefore boycott 1937 elections?**  
Core: No. It condemned the Act as inadequate and still contested; office was accepted later, conditionally.

**2. Name the provinces Congress did not run.**  
Core: Punjab (Unionist), Bengal (Krishak Praja–League), Sind (coalitions). NWFP *was* Congress-aligned — do not dump it in the “Muslim provinces rejected Congress” bin.

**3. Why is U.P. coalition refusal examinable?**  
Core: Congress majority refused a League coalition on “join the party first” terms; League heard permanent exclusion from the heartland executive.

**4. What could ministries actually touch?**  
Core: Provincial list — jails and prisoners, tenancy talk, excise/prohibition, education, local government, ordinary police under a Governor’s special-power shadow.

**5. Why resign in October 1939?**  
Core: Linlithgow took India into the Second World War without consulting the elected provincial governments or offering a war-aim that included India’s own freedom timetable.

**6. What is Day of Deliverance?**  
Core: 22 December 1939; Jinnah’s call to mark the end of Congress ministries — evidence of how poisoned the 1937–39 relationship had become.

**7. Premier names (any four).**  
Core: Pant (U.P.), Rajaji (Madras), Kher (Bombay), S.K. Sinha (Bihar), R.S. Shukla (C.P.), Biswanath Das (Orissa), Dr Khan Sahib (NWFP), Bordoloi (Assam phase).

**8. Ethics core in one line.**  
Core: Using a colonial desk justly is a duty to the voter; leaving that desk over an undeclared war is conscience; both can be true and both have costs.
