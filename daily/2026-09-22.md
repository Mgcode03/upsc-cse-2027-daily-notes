# BLOCK A — Tripuri, Bose, Forward Bloc (50 minutes)

## 1. Two Congresses already living in one party

By 1938 the Indian National Congress is a mass party that has just tasted office. That creates two temperaments:

- **The office temperament.** Rajaji, Pant, Patel, the parliamentary wing: keep the organisation intact, do not smash the ministry experiment until the war question forces a smash, treat Gandhi as the moral court of appeal.
- **The left-nationalist temperament.** Subhas Chandra Bose, Congress Socialists, a slice of youth and Bengal: the 1935 Act is a cage, office is a trap, Britain is weak in a coming European war, India should use the weakness for a harder push — not a polite resignation letter.

Bose is not a footnote. He is twice Congress President. First, **Haripura, February 1938**, he is elected with Gandhi’s camp not yet in open war with him. At Haripura he talks of a planned economy, a national planning committee idea (Nehru will chair planning talk), and a more militant national line. The party still looks united in the photograph.

**Teaching sentence:** Haripura is Bose as **President inside the family**. Tripuri is Bose as **President against the family**.

## 2. The 1939 election that should not have been an election

In early 1939 Bose seeks a second term. Gandhi’s camp puts up **Pattabhi Sitaramayya**. This is not a polite preference. It is a test of whether the President of Congress is a clerk of the High Command or a leader with his own majority of delegates.

Bose wins. The margin is real. Gandhi then issues a public sentence that Sitaramayya’s defeat is **his own defeat**. That sentence is the political earthquake. A party that treats Gandhi as the unwritten constitution cannot have a President whom Gandhi has just declared a personal loss.

At **Tripuri (March 1939)**, near Jabalpur in the Central Provinces, the session becomes a loyalty trial. Govind Ballabh Pant moves a resolution that the President must appoint a Working Committee **in accordance with Gandhi’s wishes**. That is the operational meaning of the fight: who names the cabinet of the party.

Bose is ill, isolated, and asked to become a President who cannot choose his own colleagues. He resigns the presidency in **April 1939**. **Rajendra Prasad** becomes President in his place — the organisational restoration of the Gandhi–Patel–Prasad line.

**Prelims trap:** “Gandhi defeated Bose in a delegate vote at Tripuri.” False. Bose had already won the presidential contest; Tripuri is the aftermath that makes the win unusable.

**Prelims trap:** “Nehru walked out with Bose.” False. Nehru is left of the parliamentary right but does not follow Bose out of the party command.

## 3. Forward Bloc and the road out of India

In **May 1939** Bose and his supporters form the **Forward Bloc** inside Congress first, as a left platform. The High Command does not treat a parallel bloc as indoor sport. Disciplinary action follows. By 1940 Bose is out of the official Congress command structure.

Why this matters beyond biography:

1. Congress loses its most war-minded mass leader just as a world war starts.
2. Bengal’s radical youth now have a name that is not Gandhi’s.
3. The later Indian National Army story only makes sense if you know Bose did not invent militancy in Singapore. He left an Indian party fight first.
4. Gandhi’s hold on Congress after Tripuri is **organisational**, not unanimous affection. That hold will matter in 1942 when the same machine must decide whether to launch Quit India.

Bose is arrested, released, then disappears from Calcutta in **January 1941**, reaching Germany via Kabul and the Soviet route. Do not spend today on INA battles; park one sentence: the man who lost Tripuri will next try to internationalise India’s war. Tomorrow-and-after files own 1941–45 abroad.

**Teaching sentence:** Tripuri decides that **Congress will fight the empire Gandhi’s way or not as one party**.

# BLOCK B — Lahore Resolution, 23 March 1940 (55 minutes)

## 1. What the League is after the ministries resign

October 1939 created a vacuum in eight provinces and a talking point for Jinnah: Congress Raj had been a warning. League sessions through late 1939 and early 1940 convert grievance into **doctrine**.

The session that matters is at **Lahore, March 1940**. The resolution adopted on **23 March 1940** is what later popular speech calls the **Pakistan Resolution**. The word **Pakistan** is not the operational noun inside every line of the original text you must respect. The operational idea is: geographically **contiguous units** where Muslims are a majority shall be grouped as **Independent States** with autonomy and sovereignty.

Read that slowly.

- It is **not** yet a neatly drawn two-dominion map with a corridor and a nameplate.
- It **is** a rejection of a single Indian nation in which Muslims are a permanent minority under majority rule.
- It speaks of **states** in the plural in the original wording — a fact Prelims loves and Mains must not over-smooth. Later League politics tightens the idea toward one Pakistan; 1940 is the territorial turn, not the final cartography.

**Teaching sentence:** 1940 is when a **cultural-political minority claim becomes a map claim**.

## 2. Why Lahore, why that year

Three pressures coincide:

1. **1937 cabinet arithmetic**, especially U.P.: a majority party said join us or stay out. Separate electorates had given Muslims a legislative room; they had not given an executive room as of right.
2. **The federation that never started.** The 1935 Centre was supposed to be a princely-plus-province house. It failed. There is no working all-India bargain to defend.
3. **War.** Britain needs Muslim-majority army belts and League non-obstruction. A party that can threaten to withhold wartime cooperation has leverage it did not have in 1930.

Iqbal’s 1930 Allahabad presidential address had already imagined a north-western Muslim state as a cultural home. Rahmat Ali’s pamphlet word **Pakistan** (Punjab, Afghania/NWFP, Kashmir, Sind, Baluchistan) is circulating among students. Lahore 1940 is the moment the **League as an organisation** puts a territorial formula into a resolution, not the moment a poet invents a feeling.

## 3. What the resolution does not settle

- Borders of Bengal and Punjab. Both are Muslim-majority as whole provinces and internally mixed. A “contiguous Muslim unit” can mean the whole province or a sliced province. 1947 will fight that ambiguity with a razor.
- The fate of Muslims in Hindu-majority provinces — U.P., Bihar, C.P., Madras. A homeland in the north-west and east does not automatically protect a minority that stays. This is the ethical and political hole inside two-nation talk.
- Princes. Kashmir is Muslim-majority with a Hindu ruler; Hyderabad is the reverse pattern. 1940 does not solve princely sovereignty.
- Whether “Independent States” means one federation of Muslim units or several. Ambiguity is useful in 1940 and costly later.

**Prelims trap:** “Lahore Resolution demanded a single state called Pakistan with specified district borders.” Too neat. Demand the territorial principle; do not invent the Radcliffe line in 1940.

**Prelims trap:** “Congress immediately accepted the two-nation theory.” False. Congress continues to speak of one nation with safeguards. That refusal is half principle and half blindness to how 1937 felt from the other side of the cabinet table.

## 4. Two-nation theory as an argument, not a slogan

Write it as Jinnah’s brief:

- Nations are made of religion, law, culture, and historical memory, not only of a shared map.
- Hindus and Muslims are two such nations.
- In a single democracy the more numerous nation rules. Safeguards and reserved seats are gifts, not sovereignty.
- Therefore Muslim-majority regions must be **sovereign homes**, not minority corners.

Write the Congress-nationalist brief:

- A nation can be political and territorial: those who inhabit India and want a common citizenship.
- Religion is real; it is not a census of two states.
- Partition of people who share villages, rivers, and markets will create refugees, not safety.
- Minority rights belong **inside** a constitution, not in a second geography.

For Mains you hold both briefs long enough to show you understand the wound. You are not required to baptise 1940 as destiny or as a crime in one adjective.

**Teaching sentence:** Separate electorates created a **roll**; Lahore 1940 demanded a **country**.

# BLOCK C — August Offer and Individual Satyagraha (50 minutes)

## 1. Britain needs Indian men and Indian quiet

By mid-1940 Germany has overrun Western Europe. The fall of France (June 1940) makes the war existentially British. India is a reservoir of soldiers, raw material, and a base against a possible Axis reach toward the Indian Ocean. Linlithgow must keep the army recruiting and keep Congress from a mass revolt.

Congress after resignation wants a price: a wartime national government with real power, and a clear postwar promise of independence through an Indian constituent body. The League wants its own price: no settlement that treats League as a minority lobby inside a Congress nation.

## 2. The August Offer, 8 August 1940

Linlithgow’s **August Offer** is a wartime political advertisement. Own its clauses as a list, not as a paragraph of fog:

1. After the war, a **representative Indian body** would be set up to frame a new constitution. This is the closest the Offer comes to a constituent-assembly hint — still imperial in timing and supervision.
2. The **minorities** are promised that the British government will not transfer power to any system **which those minorities do not accept**. This sentence is oxygen for the League. It is a veto dressed as protection.
3. Dominion Status is the horizon language of the time; it is not immediate independence.
4. Immediate expansion of the Viceroy’s Executive Council to include more Indians, and a **War Advisory Council**.
5. No present transfer of defence or real cabinet responsibility of the kind Congress demanded.

**Why Congress rejects it.** No national government now. No clear independence now. A minority veto that can freeze any future constitution. Gandhi sees a cheque dated “after the war” written by a bank that may still own the branch.

**Why the League is warmer, then still not satisfied.** The minority-veto clause recognises that Muslims are not just another pressure group. But the Offer does not enact Lahore. Jinnah can pocket the principle and keep demanding the map.

**Teaching sentence:** August Offer sells **postwar constitution-talk plus a minority lock**, not power today.

## 3. Why Gandhi does not launch another Dandi

A full Civil Disobedience in 1940 would:

- look to many British and some Indian liberals like stabbing a wounded anti-Nazi war;
- invite a repression that Congress, out of office, cannot cushion with ministries;
- possibly slip into communal riots the party cannot police;
- contradict Gandhi’s own need to keep satyagraha **moral**, not opportunistic.

Silence is also impossible. If Congress says nothing while India is used as a barracks, the claim to speak for the nation dies.

The compromise design is **Individual Satyagraha**, launched from **October 1940**.

## 4. How Individual Satyagraha works

- One person at a time, chosen and authorised, not a crowd storming a salt work.
- The satyagrahi makes an anti-war speech: India did not consent to this war; a subject people cannot be conscripted into an imperial fight as if they were free citizens.
- The slogan-core is the **right to free speech against the war effort**, not yet “quit India.”
- The first satyagrahi is **Vinoba Bhave** (17 October 1940). **Jawaharlal Nehru** follows and is imprisoned. A roll of authorised individuals continues into 1941. About **25,000** court arrest in the campaign’s span — a serious number, still not a 1930-style national stoppage.
- Gandhi suspends and calibrates as war news and repression shift. By late 1941 the campaign is no longer the centre of gravity; the next explosion will be 1942 after Cripps fails.

**Teaching sentence:** Individual Satyagraha is **conscience with a gatekeeper** — revolt small enough to stay non-violent and large enough to refuse consent.

## 5. What 1940–41 looks like from three desks

- **Congress desk:** out of office, internally post-Tripuri, morally on record against an undeclared war, organisationally not in full revolt.
- **League desk:** not running a mass anti-war satyagraha; building the Lahore idea; offering wartime cooperation as bargaining chips; celebrating the end of Congress ministries as political luck.
- **Viceroy desk:** recruit, buy grain, expand the council with cooperative Indians, keep the railways and ports moving, wait for an American-entry war to change the global math (December 1941 is still months away when Individual Satyagraha starts).

# BLOCK D — Geography, economy, ethics, current-affairs-explained (50 minutes)

## 1. Geography you must be able to draw

**Tripuri** sits in the Central Provinces, on the road-and-rail world of Jabalpur — a Hindi-heartland session town, not a coastal presidency. Symbolism: the fight over who owns Congress is staged in the interior machine, not only in Calcutta.

**Calcutta to Kabul to Europe** is Bose’s later line. Today only mark Calcutta as the port-metropolis that can hide a disappearance, and the North-West as the old grand-trunk exit toward Afghanistan.

**Lahore, March 1940.** Capital of Unionist Punjab, canal-colony wheat belt, mixed city of Hindus, Sikhs, Muslims. Choosing Lahore for a Muslim-homeland resolution is a claim planted in a province that is **not** a simple Muslim village. Punjab’s later 1947 horror is already latent in this choice of stage.

**The two Muslim-majority belts the resolution is pointing at:**

- **North-west:** Punjab, Sind, NWFP, Baluchistan, with Kashmir as the disputed jewel. Indus and five doabs. Army recruitment geography. Canal colonies. Afghan edge.
- **East:** Bengal (and later the Assam question). Tidal delta, jute, Calcutta as a Hindu-professional and British-commercial head on a Muslim-majority body.

**The hole in the map:** U.P. and Bihar Muslims — large in number, minority in percentage, historically central to League elite politics. A Pakistan in the west and east leaves them as the community that theory cannot house.

**Princely gaps:** still no 1937-style vote in Hyderabad, Travancore, Mysore, Kashmir. Any “nation” talk in 1940 is still talk about **British-province politics** plus a wish about princes.

**Teaching sentence:** 1940 draws a **two-belt Muslim map** across a **three-community Punjab** and a **two-headed Bengal**.

## 2. Economy — war without ministries

- Recruitment and military demand begin to pull labour and grain. Full inflation and famine are 1942–43 stories; the fuse is already lit in 1940–41 procurement.
- Congress ministries have left the provincial budget desks. Governors and ICS officers run war supply. Political legitimacy of price control is weak because the popular party is in jail or in satyagraha.
- Jute (Bengal) and cotton (Bombay-Gujarat-C.P.) become strategic raw materials. A peasant does not experience “world war” as a map. He experiences a price and a levy.
- The August Offer’s expanded Executive Council is also an economic committee: Indians closer to the files, not closer to the printing of policy.
- Individual Satyagraha is cheap as disruption. That is the point. Gandhi does not try to stop the railways in 1940. The economic war effort continues. Politics is a speech and an arrest, not a general strike.

**GS-III link:** a state that demands wartime sacrifice without representative consent stores **inflation plus rage**. 1943 will invoice that storage.

## 3. Ethics — GS-IV cores

1. **Loyalty after a lost inner election.** Bose won delegates and lost the party. Is leaving to find foreign help patriotism or vanity? Write the test as **means**: does the leader still treat Indian civilians as ends, or as fuel for a personal war?
2. **Minority veto versus majority democracy.** August Offer’s minority lock can be read as justice (no constitution without consent of the threatened) or as a colonial device to never leave. Both readings belong in one paragraph.
3. **Two-nation as ethics.** If religion is treated as the only nation, a mixed village becomes a problem to be solved by movement of bodies. If religion is treated as irrelevant, a frightened minority is told its fear is imaginary. Adult answers refuse both cruelties.
4. **Individual Satyagraha and responsibility.** Choosing who may go to jail is discipline; it can also be control. Gandhi as gatekeeper of protest: moral hygiene or oligarchy of conscience?
5. **War and truth-telling.** Speaking against a war you did not declare is free speech. Speaking in a way that hopes for Axis victory is a different moral object. Bose and Gandhi diverge here. Name the divergence without cartooning either man.

## 4. Current-affairs-explained — living structures

- **Inner-party presidential contests** that the high command then hollows out remain an Indian political habit. Tripuri is the textbook of “you won the vote, we keep the machine.”
- **Homeland resolutions** still appear wherever a group decides that safeguards inside a state are not enough. 1940 is the Indian origin-story of that move from reservation to territory.
- **Individual protest versus mass protest** is a live tactical choice in any democracy: one authorised speaker at a time keeps the moral frame; it also keeps the state comfortable. Ask, every time you see a token protest, whether it is courage or containment.
- **Wartime executive expansion** without legislative transfer is the colonial ancestor of “we will consult you on the war cabinet but not share the war power.” Modern emergency and ordinance politics rhyme; they do not photocopy.
- **23 March** is observed in Pakistan as a national day tied to the Lahore Resolution. Knowing why a neighbour’s calendar marks that date is GS-II neighbourhood literacy, not trivia.

# BLOCK E — CSAT with worked solutions (40 minutes)

Negative marking in Prelims CSAT is one-third of the marks assigned to the question. These are practice items. Show every step.

## Q1. Percentages

A province’s wartime grain levy is raised from 12% of marketed surplus to 15% of marketed surplus. If marketed surplus itself falls by 10% because peasants withhold stock, what is the percentage change in the absolute levy collected?

**Solution.**
Let original marketed surplus = 100 units.
Original levy = 12% of 100 = 12.
New surplus = 90.
New levy = 15% of 90 = 13.5.
Change = 13.5 − 12 = +1.5.
Percentage change = (1.5 / 12) × 100 = **12.5% increase**.

**Teaching cut:** two percentages never add. Always restore a base of 100, apply the second change to the new base.

## Q2. Ratios

Delegates at a party session vote 1580 : 1375 between two presidential candidates. If 5% of the winner’s votes and 8% of the loser’s votes are later declared invalid, find the new ratio of valid votes, simplified.

**Solution.**
Winner valid = 0.95 × 1580 = 1501.
Loser valid = 0.92 × 1375 = 1265.
Ratio = 1501 : 1265.
Divide both by 7: 214.428… — 7 is not a clean factor of 1501. Check gcd.
1501 = 1501 ÷ 1501 = 1 × 1501; 1501 ÷ 19 = 79; 1265 ÷ 5 = 253; 253 ÷ 11 = 23. No common factor with 1501 (1501 = 19 × 79). So **1501 : 1265** is already simplest.

If an option set forces approximation, do not invent a factor. Exact ratio is the answer.

## Q3. Time and work

Three satyagrahis A, B, C can write and deliver authorised speeches in 10, 12 and 15 days respectively if each works alone at a campaign desk. They work together for 2 days, then C is arrested. A and B finish the remaining desk-work. How many days in total does the desk take?

**Solution.**
Rates: A = 1/10, B = 1/12, C = 1/15 per day.
Together = (6 + 5 + 4) / 60 = 15/60 = 1/4 per day.
In 2 days they finish 1/2.
Remaining = 1/2.
A + B = (6 + 5)/60 = 11/60 per day.
Time for remainder = (1/2) ÷ (11/60) = 30/11 days.
Total time = 2 + 30/11 = **52/11 days = 4 8/11 days**.

## Q4. Syllogism

Statements: All resolutions are maps. Some maps are vetoes. No veto is a ministry.
Conclusions: (I) Some resolutions are vetoes. (II) No ministry is a resolution. (III) Some maps are not ministries.

**Solution.**
All resolutions ⊂ maps. Some maps ∩ vetoes. Vetoes ∩ ministries = empty.
(I) does not follow: the maps that are vetoes need not be the maps that are resolutions.
(II) does not follow: resolutions could sit in the part of maps that is outside vetoes, so they may or may not touch ministries; we have no line between resolutions and ministries.
(III) follows: those maps that are vetoes cannot be ministries, so some maps are not ministries.
Answer: **only III**.

## Q5. Ratios applied to seats

A restricted electorate of 30 lakh is split among three communal rolls in the ratio 5 : 3 : 2. If the franchise is later widened so that the first group grows 20%, the second 10%, and the third 50%, what is the new ratio?

**Solution.**
Old parts: 5k, 3k, 2k (let k be the common multiple).
New: 5k × 1.2 = 6k; 3k × 1.1 = 3.3k; 2k × 1.5 = 3k.
Multiply by 10 to clear the decimal: 60 : 33 : 30.
Divide by 3: **20 : 11 : 10**.

# BLOCK F — Active recall and model cores (25 minutes)

Close the notes. Answer in one or two sentences. Then check the core.

**1. What did Bose win and what did he lose in 1939?**
Core: He won the presidential contest against Pattabhi Sitaramayya; he lost the Working Committee, because Tripuri bound him to Gandhi’s wishes and he resigned. Rajendra Prasad took the chair.

**2. What did the Lahore Resolution actually demand?**
Core: Grouping of geographically contiguous Muslim-majority units as Independent States with sovereignty — a territorial two-nation turn, not yet a finished district map named in every clause as one Pakistan.

**3. Why did Congress reject the August Offer?**
Core: No real national government during the war, only a postwar constitution body under imperial timing, plus a minority veto on any future transfer.

**4. Why Individual Satyagraha instead of mass Civil Disobedience?**
Core: To refuse consent to a war India did not declare, without appearing to knife an anti-Nazi struggle or unleashing a crowd Congress could not keep non-violent.

**5. Who was the first individual satyagrahi?**
Core: Vinoba Bhave, 17 October 1940; Nehru followed and was imprisoned.

**6. Why is NWFP a problem for lazy two-nation talk in 1937–40?**
Core: A Muslim-majority frontier province had already run a Congress-aligned ministry. Muslim majority did not automatically mean League ministry.

**7. What fiscal fact follows when popular ministries resign in a coming war?**
Core: Levy, procurement and inflation files return to Governors just as demand spikes — legitimacy of extraction falls while extraction rises.

**Mains 10-mark spine (write this once in the margin):**
1939–41 converted three frustrations into three doctrines: Bose’s that Congress had become a Gandhi machine; Jinnah’s that minority dignity required territory; Gandhi’s that truth-force must stay individual until the nation was ready for a larger fire. Britain answered with an August postcard of postwar constitution-talk and a wartime minority lock. The postcard did not buy Congress; the lock did not give the League a state; the individual jail-goers did not stop the army. The next bargain would have to be larger. That bargain’s name, in 1942, is Cripps — and its failure’s name is Quit India.
