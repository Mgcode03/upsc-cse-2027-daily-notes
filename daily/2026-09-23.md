# BLOCK A — Why 1942 is not 1940 (40 minutes)

## 1. The map that changed between Individual Satyagraha and Cripps

In October 1940 the war was still, for most Indians, a European and North African story. By early 1942 it is an Asian story.

Own this sequence as a chain, not as trivia:

- **7 December 1941:** Japan attacks Pearl Harbor. The United States enters the war. Britain is no longer fighting alone, but the Pacific and Indian Ocean are now a Japanese theatre.
- **December 1941 – February 1942:** Japan overruns Malaya. **Singapore falls on 15 February 1942.** Singapore was the symbol of British invincibility east of Suez. Tens of thousands of Indian troops in British Indian Army units become prisoners. That prison camp is one seed of the later Indian National Army. Do not fight INA battles today; park the seed.
- **March 1942:** Japan takes Rangoon. Burma’s rice and the overland road toward China collapse. Refugees — Indian traders, clerks, labourers — walk back toward Assam and Bengal. The war is no longer a newspaper. It is a column of people on a hill path.
- The Andaman and Nicobar islands come under Japanese occupation later in 1942. The Bay of Bengal is no longer a British lake.

**Teaching sentence:** After Singapore, India is not a **safe rear**; it is the **next possible front**.

## 2. Three pressures on London

1. **Military.** A Japanese army in Burma can look at Imphal, Kohima, Chittagong and Calcutta. The eastern railway, the Assam tea gardens, and the Calcutta industrial belt are suddenly strategic.
2. **American and Chinese.** Roosevelt wants a political gesture that keeps Indian cooperation and keeps Chiang Kai-shek’s China supplied. A sulking India is a bad Allied poster.
3. **Indian.** Congress is out of office but not dead. The League has Lahore as doctrine. Princely India is still a third map. A vague August Offer is no longer enough packaging.

Churchill does not convert to Indian independence. He authorises a mission because the alliance and the eastern front demand a show of movement.

**Prelims trap:** “Cripps came because Churchill wanted to transfer power in 1942.” False. Cripps came because the war cabinet needed Indian calm and Allied optics. Churchill’s private faith remains imperial.

## 3. Who Stafford Cripps is

Stafford Cripps is a Labour lawyer in Churchill’s wartime coalition, known as a left-leaning negotiator, sent in **March 1942** with a draft declaration. He talks to Congress, the League, Ambedkar, princely representatives, Sikhs. The talks are real. The draft is tightly boxed from London.

**Teaching sentence:** Cripps can **explain** a formula; he cannot **enlarge** it without Churchill.

# BLOCK B — The Cripps offer, clause by clause (55 minutes)

## 1. What the draft actually said

Memorise the offer as five working parts.

**Part 1 — After the war, Dominion Status.** India would be offered dominion status — the language of the Statute of Westminster world: a self-governing member of the Commonwealth, with the right to walk further. This is stronger horizon-language than the August Offer, still not “independence this month.”

**Part 2 — An Indian constitution-making body after the war.** Provinces and Indian States would send representatives to frame a new constitution. This is the constituent-assembly idea in imperial wrapping: after victory, not during the bombing.

**Part 3 — The right of a province not to accede.** Any province that did not accept the new constitution could retain its existing position and negotiate a separate arrangement with Britain. This is the clause Congress reads as a **partition door** and the League reads as **not yet Pakistan**.

**Part 4 — Princes.** Indian States would be represented in the constitution-making body, but their accession logic remains princely. The people of the States are not the unit; the ruler is still the legal person.

**Part 5 — During the war, the British keep defence.** Indian political leaders may join an expanded Executive Council, but the Commander-in-Chief and the defence of India stay British. There is no national government with authority over the army today.

**Teaching sentence:** Cripps offers **tomorrow’s dominion with an exit door for provinces, and today’s defence lock**.

## 2. Why Congress says no

Write the Congress brief as Gandhi, Nehru, and Azad would have felt it in the same week:

- **No power now.** If Japan is at the gate, the people who claim to speak for India want a real share of defence and civil authority, not a seat at a consultative table.
- **The provincial opt-out.** A clause that lets a province walk away is, in 1942 ears, a British blessing on the Lahore logic. Congress still speaks of one nation. It will not sign a document that teaches provinces how to secede before a constitution exists.
- **Princes over people.** A constituent body that treats Hyderabad or Travancore as a ruler’s file is not a people’s assembly.
- Gandhi’s reported metaphor — a **post-dated cheque** on a crashing bank — is the exam line. Use it as a summary of timing, not as a joke. The bank is an empire that may lose the east; the date on the cheque is “after the war.”

Azad is Congress President in this phase. Nehru wants Allied cooperation against fascism but not as a voiceless recruiting sergeant. The Working Committee rejects the draft.

## 3. Why the League says no (for the opposite reason)

Jinnah’s problem is not that the opt-out exists. His problem is that the opt-out is **provincial**, not **communal-national**.

- A Hindu-majority province cannot be expected to opt into Pakistan.
- A Muslim-majority province might opt out of an Indian union — but Punjab and Bengal are internally mixed. Provincial option does not guarantee the two-belt map of 1940.
- The draft does not name the League as a co-equal nation. It treats Muslims as a possible provincial majority, which is less than Lahore.

So both big parties reject the same paper for opposite readings of the same clause.

**Prelims trap:** “League accepted Cripps and Congress refused.” False. Both refused. Their refusals are not the same sentence.

**Prelims trap:** “Cripps promised immediate independence and a national cabinet with full defence powers.” False. Defence stayed British; independence was postwar dominion language.

## 4. Other desks at the same table

- **Ambedkar** and depressed-class politics want safeguards that a hurried wartime bargain between Congress and Raj must not swallow. The Cripps talks are another reminder that “India” is not two parties.
- **Sikh politics** in Punjab hear the provincial-option clause as a threat: a Muslim-majority Punjab walking out would leave Sikhs inside a new majority they did not choose.
- **Princes** like a scheme that still talks to rulers. They do not like a scheme that might later be read as popular sovereignty inside the State.

**Teaching sentence:** Cripps fails because it tries to satisfy **war cabinet, Congress unity, League territory, princes, and minorities** with one short draft.

## 5. What failure does to 1942

Once Cripps flies home in early April, the political middle disappears.

- Britain will not reopen the draft under Indian pressure while Japanese armies sit in Burma.
- Congress cannot go back to Individual Satyagraha as if Singapore never fell. The moral claim “we did not consent to this war” now sits beside the physical claim “the war is in Assam’s waiting room.”
- Gandhi moves from gated protest to a demand that the British **leave**, so that a free India can either resist Japan or negotiate — as a nation, not as a barracks.

That demand becomes the August resolution.

# BLOCK C — Quit India: resolution, vacuum, fire (55 minutes)

## 1. The Wardha to Bombay road

Through July 1942 the Congress Working Committee at Wardha shapes a resolution. The All India Congress Committee meets in **Bombay**. On **8 August 1942** the AICC adopts what the country will call the **Quit India Resolution**.

Own the inner logic, not only the slogan:

- British rule in India must end **immediately**.
- A free India will resist fascism with the full energy of a free people; a subject India cannot be asked to die for a freedom it is denied.
- If the British go, Congress is willing to negotiate a provisional government that includes others — the sentence is broader than “Congress Raj tomorrow.” In practice the League hears only eviction.
- Gandhi’s public charge to the country is **“Do or Die.”** It is not a licence to kill. It is a licence to accept the last full cost of non-cooperation.

**Teaching sentence:** Quit India is **leave now**, not **leave after a committee**.

## 2. The night the leadership vanishes

The government does not wait for a second Dandi march to be organised. In the early hours of **9 August 1942** Gandhi, Nehru, Patel, Azad and the Working Committee are arrested. Gandhi is held at Aga Khan Palace, Pune. Other leaders go to Ahmednagar Fort and elsewhere.

This is the defining administrative fact of 1942: **the movement is decapitated before it is organised.**

What follows is therefore not a replica of 1930.

- 1930 had routes, volunteers, ashram discipline, and a leader who could start and stop.
- 1942 has a slogan, a vacuum, local Congress workers, students, peasants, mill hands, and in some districts men who have waited years to cut a wire.

**Prelims trap:** “Gandhi led street processions through Bombay all autumn 1942.” False. He is inside Aga Khan Palace. The streets do not have him.

## 3. What “leaderless” actually looked like

Name the pattern, then name a few places. Do not pretend one district is the whole country.

**Pattern A — Hartal and procession.** Cities close shops, students leave class, crowds face lathi and firing. Bombay, Calcutta, Delhi, Patna, many mofussil towns.

**Pattern B — The nervous system of the Raj.** Rails, telegraph wires, post offices, police stations. A war state lives on the line from Calcutta to the Assam front. Cutting that line is both nationalist sabotage and a military problem. The government treats it as the second.

**Pattern C — Parallel authority in pockets.** In parts of **Bihar, eastern U.P., Midnapore (Bengal), Satara (Maharashtra), Ballia**, local activists try to run a few days or weeks of their own thana and tax logic. These are real and limited. They are not a national alternative government.

**Pattern D — Underground Congress.** Socialists and younger workers who escaped the first sweep — names you should at least recognise: Jayaprakash Narayan, Ram Manohar Lohia, Aruna Asaf Ali, Sucheta Kripalani, later a scatter of bomb-and-radio work. This is the wing that says non-violence cannot be the only grammar once the Mahatma is caged. Gandhi in prison does not authorise their violence. The street does not always wait for authorisation.

**Teaching sentence:** Quit India is a **national slogan with local wars**, because the national office was locked on day one.

## 4. Repression as policy

The wartime Raj has Defence of India rules, troops, aircraft in some areas, and no ministry in the Congress provinces to argue for restraint.

- Mass arrests run into tens of thousands.
- Firing on crowds is repeated in several provinces.
- Collective fines hit villages near sabotaged lines.
- Press is gagged; the official story is that Congress stabbed a war for freedom.
- By late 1942 the open movement is broken as a coordinated national event. Sporadic underground work continues into 1943–44.

**Mahatma Gandhi’s fast, February–March 1943**, at Aga Khan Palace, is a later moral sequel: he rejects the charge that he ordered violence and fasts against the government’s indictment. Kasturba Gandhi dies in detention in February 1944. Gandhi is released in **May 1944** on medical grounds. Those dates belong on the same reel as August 1942; they are the price-ticket of the slogan.

## 5. Who did not join, and why that matters

- **Muslim League** does not turn Quit India into a League campaign. Jinnah calls it a Congress leap. League organisation uses the years 1942–45 to deepen the Lahore claim while Congress leaders sit in forts. This political asymmetry is one reason 1945–47 is not a replay of 1937.
- **Communist Party of India**, after the Soviet Union is invaded in 1941, treats the world war as a people’s war against fascism and does not throw its machine behind Quit India. Remember the date-logic: ideology follows the European front, not the Bombay resolution.
- **Hindu Mahasabha** and a slice of the princely-loyal and job-holding middle class stay out or oppose.
- **Punjab Unionist** and much of the army-recruitment belt do not become the heartland of 1942 the way Bihar and the eastern United Provinces do.

**Prelims trap:** “Quit India was a united action of Congress, League, and Communists.” False. It is a Congress-called storm with a large popular surge and large absences.

**Teaching sentence:** 1942 fills jails with Congress and leaves the League **in the open** — that fact is politics, not a footnote.

# BLOCK D — Geography, economy, ethics, current-affairs-explained (50 minutes)

## 1. Geography you must be able to draw

Draw three layers on one mental map.

**Layer 1 — The Japanese approach.** From Malaya and Singapore across the Bay; through Burma up the river corridors toward the Chin-Naga-Manipur hills. The names for later battles — **Imphal** and **Kohima**, 1944 — already sit on this approach road. Chittagong and the Assam rail-river complex are the Indian end of the same corridor. Calcutta is the industrial and port brain of the east.

**Layer 2 — The Quit India heat map (open phase, August–September 1942).** Heaviest disruption: Bihar, eastern United Provinces, Bombay city and mill belt, Midnapore, parts of Madras Presidency and Central Provinces. Not empty, but less central: much of Punjab, NWFP politics of a different grain, many princely interiors.

**Layer 3 — Detention geography.** Aga Khan Palace at Pune for Gandhi. Ahmednagar Fort for a block of the Working Committee. This is a west-Indian prison map for an all-India command.

**Bombay as stage.** The resolution is passed in a western port-metropolis that is also a mill city. The first crowds and the first arrests are in the same streets that once funded Tilak and later funded textiles for war.

**Midnapore** deserves one extra line: a delta-edge district that tries local self-rule under repression, then meets cyclone and later famine. Geography here is water plus administration plus hunger. The 1943 famine file will collect the hunger; today you only mark that Midnapore is already political tinder in 1942.

**Teaching sentence:** 1942 has an **eastern war frontier** and a **Gangetic revolt belt**; they are not the same line, but the Raj must hold both.

## 2. Economy — war demand without consent

- Defence spending, Allied demand, and disrupted Southeast Asian rice (Burma was a rice tap) push prices. 1942 is the acceleration year toward the Bengal famine of 1943. Do not collapse the two years into one cliché; do see the fuse.
- Rail priority goes to troops and war goods toward the east. Civilian grain movement becomes a political act.
- Mill labour in Bombay faces wage lag against prices. A strike in a war mill is both bread politics and a security file.
- Cutting rails in Bihar is cheap for a crowd and expensive for a front. That is why repression is military in tone even when the target is a village boy with pliers.
- Cripps’s failed “national government” also means there is no popular cabinet to defend rationing as shared sacrifice. Sacrifice without voice is experienced as loot.

**GS-III link:** wartime inflation plus broken supply plus lost political legitimacy is the textbook of how a food crisis becomes a famine. Park the full 1943 mechanism for the next hunger file; keep the 1942 sentence.

## 3. Ethics — GS-IV cores

1. **Means when the leader is removed.** If you preach non-violence and then cannot police the movement, are you still responsible for the cut rail? Gandhi says he did not order sabotage. The peasant who heard “Do or Die” says he was not given a second manual. Write both claims.
2. **War against fascism versus war against empire.** Nehru hates fascism and still refuses a voiceless war. Bose will seek Axis help (next files). The ethical test is whether Indian lives are treated as ends. Both men can fail that test in different ways. Name the test; do not canonise one and demonise the other in a single adjective.
3. **Post-dated justice.** A promise after victory is easy for the strong. Cripps is the case study of **justice delayed dressed as procedure**.
4. **Minority absence.** A mass revolt that a large religious-political organisation sits out will later be read as proof of two nations. Was League non-participation prudence, communal strategy, or both? Adult answers allow mixture.
5. **Obedience of the soldier.** Indian troops fight Japan in 1944 after some of their brothers joined INA from Singapore camps. Conscience versus oath is the GS-IV hinge. Today only plant the hinge; do not finish the INA trial story.

## 4. Current-affairs-explained — living structures

- **Decapitation as a state tactic.** Arrest the committee first, then call the crowd a riot. 1942 is the Indian classic of that sequence. Any later emergency politics rhymes here.
- **Opt-out clauses in constitutional design.** Cripps’s provincial option is the ancestor of every federal draft that tries to buy unity by advertising an exit. Exits advertised too early become maps.
- **Leaderless digital crowds** are a twenty-first-century cousin of August 1942: a slogan travels faster than a steward. The ethical problem is the same — who can stop what who started.
- **Frontier logistics.** Assam-Manipur still sit on the memory of 1942–44 as a war corridor. When you read a modern connectivity project in the Northeast, the older military geography is the first overlay, not the only one.

# BLOCK E — CSAT with worked solutions (40 minutes)

Q1. A war depot issues 8,000 bags of grain in August. In September issues rise by 25%. In October they fall by 20% of September’s figure. October issues are what percent of August issues?

**Work.** September = 8000 × 1.25 = 10,000. October = 10,000 × 0.80 = 8,000. October / August = 8000/8000 = 100%.  
**Answer: 100%.**

Q2. A rail section of 240 km is guarded by posts every 15 km including both ends. How many posts?

**Work.** Number of gaps = 240/15 = 16. Posts = gaps + 1 = 17.  
**Answer: 17.**

Q3. Three underground printers A, B, C can run a pamphlet in 12, 18 and 36 hours alone. Working together, how long?

**Work.** Rates: 1/12 + 1/18 + 1/36. LCM 36: 3/36 + 2/36 + 1/36 = 6/36 = 1/6. Time = 6 hours.  
**Answer: 6 hours.**

Q4. In a district, the ratio of arrested students to peasants is 2 : 5. If 210 peasants are arrested, students arrested are?

**Work.** Students = (2/5) × 210 = 84.  
**Answer: 84.**

Q5. A constable walks 3 km east, 4 km north, then 3 km west. Distance from start?

**Work.** East and west cancel. Net 4 km north.  
**Answer: 4 km.**

Q6. Statements: All satyagrahis are prisoners. Some prisoners are students. Conclusions: (I) Some satyagrahis are students. (II) Some students are prisoners.

**Work.** I does not follow (students may all lie in the non-satyagrahi part of prisoners). II follows from the second statement.  
**Answer: Only II follows.**

Q7. A price index is 120 in March and 150 in August. Percentage rise?

**Work.** Rise = 30 on 120 = 25%.  
**Answer: 25%.**

Q8. 40% of a crowd of 2,500 are women. 15% of the women are arrested. Women arrested?

**Work.** Women = 0.4 × 2500 = 1000. Arrested = 0.15 × 1000 = 150.  
**Answer: 150.**

# BLOCK F — Active recall and model cores (25 minutes)

1. **Why did Cripps come in March 1942, not in 1940?**  
   *Core:* Singapore and Burma had fallen; the U.S. and China wanted a political gesture; India was now a possible front, not a quiet depot.

2. **Name the five working parts of the Cripps draft.**  
   *Core:* Postwar dominion; postwar constituent body; provincial right not to accede; princely representation via rulers; wartime defence remains British.

3. **Why did Congress and League both reject the same paper?**  
   *Core:* Congress heard a partition door and no power now; League heard a mere provincial option that did not enact two nations.

4. **Date and place of the Quit India resolution.**  
   *Core:* 8 August 1942, Bombay AICC; arrests from 9 August.

5. **What does “leaderless” mean in 1942?**  
   *Core:* High Command jailed on night one; local hartals, rail-cutting, and a few parallel pockets without a national stop-start switch.

6. **Who stayed out and why it matters for 1945–47?**  
   *Core:* League (and CPI after 1941) did not run the storm; Congress emptied into jails; League organisation stayed in the open.

7. **Gandhi’s phrase and what it is not.**  
   *Core:* “Do or Die” is last-cost non-cooperation, not a written order to derail trains.

8. **One eastern geographic sentence.**  
   *Core:* Japanese Burma looks at the Imphal–Kohima–Chittagong–Calcutta corridor while Bihar cuts the rail that feeds that corridor.
